# P-02: (8.3) the graph $G_F$ of a syntactic operation

## The paper

### Statement

(8.3)

$$
G_F(\bar e, d) \quad :\Longleftrightarrow \quad
\exists s \in \omega\ \exists \tau \in \omega\
\bigl(\mathrm{DerSeq}(\bar e, s) \wedge \mathrm{Trace}_F(\bar e, d, s, \tau)\bigr)
$$

Here $\mathrm{DerSeq}(\bar e, s)$ says that $s$ codes the derivations of all input formulas
together, and $\mathrm{Trace}_F(\bar e, d, s, \tau)$ says that $\tau$ satisfies the local
recursion rules of $F$ and gives $d$ at the root. The conclusion is $G_F \in \Delta_0$.

The paper lists the following fourteen operations as covered by this schema.

free variables, syntactic depth, subformulas, the set of bound variables, the test for the
strict alternating block forms $\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_q}, \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Pi_q}$,
variable renaming, substitution, avoidance of bound variable capture, relativization, the
variable list $\mathrm{Vars}$ and the body $\mathrm{Body}$ of the outermost quantifier block,
dualization, padding, finite conjunction and disjunction, and the test for an instance of an
axiom schema

### Proof

Free variables, syntactic depth, subformulas, the set of bound variables, the test for the
strict alternating block forms $\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_q}$, $\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Pi_q}$,
variable renaming, substitution, avoidance of bound variable capture, relativization, the
variable list $\mathrm{Vars}$ and the body $\mathrm{Body}$ of the outermost quantifier block,
dualization, padding, finite conjunction and disjunction, and the test for an instance of an
axiom schema are all determined by a deterministic recursion on the finite syntax tree. For
each operation $F$, write its graph as

$$G_F(\bar e, d) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists s \in \omega\ \exists \tau \in \omega\ \bigl(\mathrm{DerSeq}(\bar e, s) \wedge \mathrm{Trace}_F(\bar e, d, s, \tau)\bigr).$$

Here $\mathrm{DerSeq}(\bar e, s)$ says that $s$ codes the derivations of all input formulas
together, and $\mathrm{Trace}_F(\bar e, d, s, \tau)$ says that $\tau$ satisfies the local
recursion rules of $F$ and gives $d$ at the root. The local recursion rules are finite
conditions comparing only the tag of each node, the indices of its children, and the values
already computed at the children. Hence all quantification in (8.3) can be bounded by
$s, \tau, \omega$ and the components of their finite sequences, and $G_F$ is $\Delta_0$. By
finite induction on the position in the derivation, the output is unique, and $\mathrm{KP}$
proves, by finite recursion on the naturals and $\Delta_0$-Collection, the existence of a
computation sequence $\tau$ and of the output for an appropriate input. Therefore every
syntactic predicate and every graph of a syntactic operation used in this paper is a literal
$\Delta_0$ relation with $\omega$ as a parameter under the single coding fixed here. Since the
outputs are natural numbers as well, the output quantification introduced when eliminating the
syntactic function symbols into the pure language is bounded by $\omega$ and does not increase
the Lévy complexity.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

The derivation $\mathrm{DerSeq}_w(s)$ is taken from [P-01](P-01.md) as it stands. The local
recursion rules are taken as three predicates given as parameters.

- $\mathcal{A}(w,p,e,v)$: the rule giving the value $v$ at an atomic node with input code $e$
- $\mathcal{I}(w,p,v_1,v_2,v)$: the rule giving $v$ at a $\to$ node from the children's values
  $v_1,v_2$
- $\mathcal{U}(w,p,i,v_1,v)$: the rule giving $v$ at a $\forall v_i$ node from the bound
  variable $i$ and the child's value $v_1$

Here $p$ is the parameter of the operation (a renaming map, a substitution pair, and so on).

**The computation sequence**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Trace}_w^{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{U}}(p,s,\tau)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Fun}(\tau)\ \wedge \\
&\forall k\,\forall e\ \Bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \to
 \exists v\ \bigl(\langle k,v\rangle \in \tau \wedge \Psi(k,e,v)\bigr)\Bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Psi(k,e,v)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathcal{A}(w,p,e,v) \\
\vee\ &\exists k_1 \in k\ \exists k_2 \in k\ \exists e_1\,e_2\,v_1\,v_2\
 \bigl(\langle k_1,e_1\rangle \in s \wedge \langle k_2,e_2\rangle \in s \wedge
 \langle k_1,v_1\rangle \in \tau \wedge \langle k_2,v_2\rangle \in \tau \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad
 \wedge\ e = \langle\bar 3,\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathcal{I}(w,p,v_1,v_2,v)\bigr) \\
\vee\ &\exists k_1 \in k\ \exists e_1\,v_1\ \exists i \in w\
 \bigl(\langle k_1,e_1\rangle \in s \wedge \langle k_1,v_1\rangle \in \tau \\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad
 \wedge\ e = \langle\bar 4,\langle i,e_1\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathcal{U}(w,p,i,v_1,v)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

**The graph**:

$$
G_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{U}}(p,e,d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\exists s \in h\ \exists \tau \in h\
\bigl(\mathrm{DerSeq}_w(s) \wedge \mathrm{Trace}_w^{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{U}}(p,s,\tau)
\wedge \exists k\ (\langle k,e\rangle \in s \wedge \langle k,d\rangle \in \tau)\bigr).
$$

There is one claim to prove.

$$
\mathcal{A},\ \mathcal{I},\ \mathcal{U} \text{ are } \Delta_0
\ \Longrightarrow\
G_{\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{U}} \text{ is } \Delta_0 \text{ over the support } \{h,w,p,e,d\}.
$$

"$\mathcal{A}$ is $\Delta_0$" means that when four distinct variable indices $a,b,c,d$ are
assigned, a $\Delta_0$ formula over the support $\{a,b,c,d\}$ represents
$\mathcal{A}(v_a,v_b,v_c,v_d)$ ($\mathcal{I}$ and $\mathcal{U}$ have five variables).

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) The computation sequence is $\Delta_0$

The paper settles it with the single sentence "all quantification in (8.3) can be bounded by
$s,\tau,\omega$ and the components of their finite sequences". In Lean a sequence is a set of
Kuratowski pairs, so **extracting a component is a descent along $\in$**, and the two rewriting
lemmas of [P-01](P-01.md) are used repeatedly.

$$
\forall k\,\forall e\ \bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \to P(k,e)\bigr)
\iff
\forall p \in s\ \forall q \in p\ \forall k \in q\ \forall q' \in p\ \forall e \in q'\
\bigl(p = \langle k,e\rangle \to P(k,e)\bigr)
$$

$$
\exists e\ \bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \wedge P(e)\bigr)
\iff
\exists p \in s\ \exists q \in p\ \exists e \in q\
\bigl(p = \langle k,e\rangle \wedge P(e)\bigr)
$$

The second disjunct of $\Psi$ has two pairs on the $s$ side ($e_1,e_2$) and two on the $\tau$
side ($v_1,v_2$), each needing a three-step descent (pair $\to$ outer pair $\to$ component).
The third disjunct has one on each side. The outer $\forall k\forall e$ needs a five-step
descent. As a result, 23 auxiliary variables are needed to assemble one $\Delta_0$ formula. The
order of assembly is from the inside out, as follows.

**The second disjunct** (a $\to$ node). Write the pairs inside $s$ as
$P_1 = \langle k_1,e_1\rangle$ and $P_2 = \langle k_2,e_2\rangle$, and those inside $\tau$ as
$R_1 = \langle k_1,v_1\rangle$ and $R_2 = \langle k_2,v_2\rangle$. The core is the conjunction
of six,

$$
P_1 = \langle k_1,e_1\rangle \wedge R_1 = \langle k_1,v_1\rangle \wedge
P_2 = \langle k_2,e_2\rangle \wedge R_2 = \langle k_2,v_2\rangle \wedge
e = \langle\bar 3,\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathcal{I}(w,p,v_1,v_2,v)
$$

and the bounded quantifiers are put on it in this order:

$$
\exists v_2 \in S_2,\ \exists S_2 \in R_2,\ \exists R_2 \in \tau,\
\exists v_1 \in S_1,\ \exists S_1 \in R_1,\ \exists R_1 \in \tau,
$$
$$
\exists e_2 \in Q_2,\ \exists Q_2 \in P_2,\ \exists P_2 \in s,\
\exists e_1 \in Q_1,\ \exists Q_1 \in P_1,\ \exists P_1 \in s,\
\exists k_2 \in k,\ \exists k_1 \in k.
$$

**The third disjunct** (a $\forall$ node). The core is

$$
P_1 = \langle k_1,e_1\rangle \wedge R_1 = \langle k_1,v_1\rangle \wedge
e = \langle\bar 4,\langle i,e_1\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathcal{U}(w,p,i,v_1,v)
$$

with $\exists i \in w$, $\exists v_1 \in S_1$, $\exists S_1 \in R_1$, $\exists R_1 \in \tau$,
$\exists e_1 \in Q_1$, $\exists Q_1 \in P_1$, $\exists P_1 \in s$, $\exists k_1 \in k$ on it.

**Existence of the value.** Multiply
$\mathcal{A}(w,p,e,v) \vee (\text{second}) \vee (\text{third})$ by
$R_0 = \langle k,v\rangle$ and put $\exists v \in Q_0$, $\exists Q_0 \in R_0$,
$\exists R_0 \in \tau$ on it.

**The outside.** Take $P = \langle k,e\rangle$ as the antecedent and put
$\forall e \in Q'$, $\forall Q' \in P$, $\forall k \in Q$, $\forall Q \in P$,
$\forall P \in s$ on it, then conjoin $\mathrm{Fun}(\tau)$.

That the resulting $\Delta_0$ formula is equivalent to $\mathrm{Trace}$ is shown by applying
the two rewriting lemmas above once to the outer $\forall$ and once to the $\exists$ for the
value, and then flattening the nested descents for the second and third disjuncts. The
rightward direction of the flattening is just taking $\{x,y\} \in \langle x,y\rangle$ and
$y \in \{x,y\}$ from $\langle x,y\rangle = \{\{x\},\{x,y\}\}$.

The support becomes $\{w,p,s,\tau\}$.

#### (b) The graph is $\Delta_0$

The "same position" conjunct
$\exists k\ (\langle k,e\rangle \in s \wedge \langle k,d\rangle \in \tau)$ is bounded as well.
With the pair inside $s$ written $P = \langle k,e\rangle$ and the one inside $\tau$ written
$P' = \langle k,d\rangle$, write

$$
\exists P \in s\ \exists Q \in P\ \exists k \in Q\ \exists P' \in \tau\
\bigl(P = \langle k,e\rangle \wedge P' = \langle k,d\rangle\bigr).
$$

Here $k$ is obtained by descending from $P$, and $P'$ is bounded by $\tau$. Conjoin this with
the $\mathrm{Trace}$ of (a) and the $\mathrm{DerSeq}_w$ of [P-01](P-01.md), and put
$\exists \tau \in h$ and $\exists s \in h$ on top. The support becomes $\{h,w,p,e,d\}$.

#### (c) An instance: free variables

Make the value of the local rule two-valued. For a proposition $P$ and a value $v$, put

$$
\mathrm{Bl}(P,v) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\ (P \wedge v = \bar 1) \vee (\neg P \wedge v = \bar 0).
$$

The local rules for "the variable $x$ does not occur free" are

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nf}}(w,x,e,v) &:\Longleftrightarrow \mathrm{Atom}_w(e) \wedge \mathrm{Bl}(\mathrm{Atom}^{\neq x}_w(e),\ v), \\
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{nf}}(w,x,v_1,v_2,v) &:\Longleftrightarrow \mathrm{Bl}(v_1 = \bar 1 \wedge v_2 = \bar 1,\ v), \\
\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{nf}}(w,x,i,v_1,v) &:\Longleftrightarrow \mathrm{Bl}(i = x \vee v_1 = \bar 1,\ v).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\mathrm{Atom}^{\neq x}_w(e)$ says that $e$ is an atomic formula code whose two variable
indices are both different from $x$. Since $\mathrm{Bl}$ makes a $\Delta_0$ predicate out of a
$\Delta_0$ predicate, all three are $\Delta_0$. Hence (b) gives

$$
G_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nf}},\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{nf}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{nf}}}
\text{ is } \Delta_0
$$

"in a single application". Correctness is

$$
\mathrm{NotFree}(\bar x, e) \iff
G_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{nf}},\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{nf}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{nf}}}(\bar x, e, \bar 1),
$$

where the left-hand side is defined by a derivation specific to $\mathrm{NotFree}$ (carrying no
values, each of whose terms is already a code in which $x$ is not free). The equivalence is
proved by showing each side equivalent to
$\exists\varphi\ (e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner \wedge x \notin \mathrm{fv}(\varphi))$ and
joining them.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are three differences.

### (i) The schema covers four of the fourteen operations

As the table in (d) shows, the instances of the schema are the four operations of free
variables, variable renaming, substitution and relativization. The test for
$\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_q}$, $\mathrm{Vars}/\mathrm{Body}$ and the axiom schema test have
their $\Delta_0$-ness proved separately rather than through the schema. Dualization and padding
are external recursions on $q$ and are not recursions on the syntax tree at all.

Of the four with no counterpart, avoidance of bound variable capture and the joining of finite
conjunctions and disjunctions are held up by one and the same gap. Both need the operation
shifting a fresh variable by $i \mapsto i+K$ written on codes, and there is no $\Delta_0$ graph
for addition on $\omega$. The successor on $\omega$ and the order given by $\in$ are available,
but addition is not. Making addition $\Delta_0$ by a recursion on $\omega$ in the style of the
schema of (8.3) would be 250 to 350 new lines, plus 150 beyond that. However, "the least index
above the free variables of $e$", used in padding, is chosen uniquely with bounded
quantification alone, so a scheme taking fresh variables one at a time might avoid addition.

Syntactic depth and subformulas are not built because nothing downstream asks for them.
Syntactic depth would be needed to build the truth set of [P-08](P-08.md) along the paper's
route.

### (ii) There is no existence claim

There is no counterpart to the second half of (8.3),

$\mathrm{KP}$ proves, by finite recursion on the naturals and $\Delta_0$-Collection, the
existence of a computation sequence $\tau$ and of the output for an appropriate input.

What Lean has is, for each instance, the external equivalence "if
$e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$ then $d = \ulcorner F(\varphi)\urcorner$ is the unique value of
the graph", not a derivation of the existence of $\tau$ inside $\mathrm{KP}$. The proof, too,
takes the form of explicitly constructing the computation sequence by structural induction on
$\varphi$. This is the same root as [P-04](P-04.md), and cannot be written without a proof
system for $\mathrm{KP}$.

### (iii) The bounding range is $L_\omega$ rather than $\omega$

$\exists s \in \omega\ \exists\tau \in \omega$ has become
$\exists s \in L_\omega\ \exists\tau \in L_\omega$. The reason is the same as in
[P-01](P-01.md).
