# P-04: opening of §10, $\mathrm{KP}$ and admissible ordinals

## The paper

### Statement

Three statements placed at the opening of §10.

**(i)** $\mathrm{KP}$ consists of Extensionality, Empty Set, Pairing, Union, Infinity,
$\Delta_0$-Separation, $\Delta_0$-Collection and Set Induction.

**(ii)** $\mathrm{KP}$ proves the existence of the least inductive set $\omega$.

**(iii)** An ordinal $\theta$ is called an **admissible ordinal** if
$L_\theta \models \mathrm{KP}$.

Every claim in §10 then takes the form "$\mathrm{KP}$ proves the following"
([P-05](P-05.md) to [P-08](P-08.md)).

### Proof

The paper gives a reason only for (ii).

Let $\mathrm{KP}$ consist of Extensionality, Empty Set, Pairing, Union, Infinity,
$\Delta_0$-Separation, $\Delta_0$-Collection and Set Induction [3]. Separating out the class of
all finite ordinals from the inductive set witnessing Infinity by $\Delta_0$-Separation, and
showing by Set Induction that it is contained in every inductive set, $\mathrm{KP}$ proves the
existence of the least inductive set $\omega$. An ordinal $\theta$ is called an admissible
ordinal if $L_\theta \models \mathrm{KP}$.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

**Codes of the axioms.** The eight axioms are written out as formulas of the object language
$\{\bot,=,\in,\to,\forall\}$, and a predicate $\mathrm{KPAx}(d)$ recognizing their codes is set
up. $\neg,\wedge,\vee,\leftrightarrow,\exists$ are derived.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Ext} &:\ \forall v_0\forall v_1\bigl((\forall v_2(v_2 \in v_0 \leftrightarrow v_2 \in v_1)) \to v_0 = v_1\bigr) \\
\mathrm{Emp} &:\ \exists v_0\,\forall v_1\,(v_1 \notin v_0) \\
\mathrm{Pair} &:\ \forall v_0\forall v_1\exists v_2(v_0 \in v_2 \wedge v_1 \in v_2) \\
\mathrm{Un} &:\ \forall v_0\exists v_1\forall v_2 \in v_0\ \forall v_3 \in v_2\ (v_3 \in v_1) \\
\mathrm{Inf} &:\ \exists v_0\bigl((\exists v_1 \in v_0\ \forall v_2 \in v_1\ \bot) \wedge
 \forall v_1 \in v_0\ \exists v_2 \in v_0\ (v_1 \in v_2 \wedge \forall v_3 \in v_1(v_3\in v_2) \wedge \forall v_3 \in v_2(v_3 \in v_1 \vee v_3 = v_1))\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

$\mathrm{Inf}$ says "an inductive set exists", that is, a set containing $\emptyset$ and closed
under $u \mapsto u \cup \{u\}$. Both $y = \emptyset$ and $v = u \cup \{u\}$ are written as
$\Delta_0$ formulas of $\{\in\}$.

The schemas are written directly on the codes. With $c$ the code of a $\Delta_0$ formula and
$a,b,x,y$ variable indices, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Sep}(a,b,x,y,c) &= \ulcorner \forall v_b\forall v_x\exists v_y\forall v_a
 (v_a \in v_y \leftrightarrow (v_a \in v_x \wedge c))\urcorner \\
\mathrm{Coll}(a,b,x,y,c) &= \ulcorner \forall v_x
 \bigl((\forall v_a \in v_x\ \exists v_b\ c) \to \exists v_y\forall v_a \in v_x\ \exists v_b \in v_y\ c\bigr)\urcorner \\
\mathrm{Ind}(a,b,c) &= \ulcorner \bigl(\forall v_b((\forall v_a \in v_b\ \forall v_b(v_b = v_a \to c)) \to c)\bigr) \to \forall v_b\ c\urcorner
\end{aligned}
$$

and then

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{KPAx}(d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&d = \ulcorner\mathrm{Ext}\urcorner \vee d = \ulcorner\mathrm{Emp}\urcorner \vee
 d = \ulcorner\mathrm{Pair}\urcorner \vee d = \ulcorner\mathrm{Un}\urcorner \vee
 d = \ulcorner\mathrm{Inf}\urcorner \\
\vee\ &\exists a,b,x,y \in w\ \exists c \in h\ \exists q \in h\ \Bigl(
 (\forall k \in w\ \mathrm{NotFree}(k,d)) \wedge \mathrm{Alls}(q,d)\ \wedge \\
&\quad \bigl[\bigl(\mathrm{Delta0Code}(c) \wedge \mathrm{NotFree}(y,c) \wedge
 a,b,x,y \text{ distinct} \\
&\qquad \wedge\ (q = \mathrm{Sep}(a,b,x,y,c) \vee q = \mathrm{Coll}(a,b,x,y,c))\bigr) \\
&\quad\ \ \vee \bigl(\mathrm{NotFree}(a,c) \wedge a \ne b \wedge q = \mathrm{Ind}(a,b,c)\bigr)\bigr]\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

$\mathrm{Alls}(q,d)$ says "$d$ is the code of the universal closure of $q$", and
$\forall k \in w\ \mathrm{NotFree}(k,d)$ says "$d$ is a sentence".

**Being a model** (the right-hand side of (iii)):

$$
\mathrm{KPModel}(\theta)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\forall \varphi\ \bigl(\mathrm{KPAx}(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner) \to
L_\theta \models \varphi\bigr).
$$

The axioms are sentences, so the valuation is irrelevant.

**The definition of admissibility** (the definition on the Lean side):

$$
\mathrm{Adm}(\theta)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\omega < \theta \ \wedge\ \theta \text{ is a limit} \ \wedge\ \Delta_0\text{-Collection}^{L_\theta}
$$

Here the third conjunct is a meta-level universal quantification over the class of $\Delta_0$
predicates. For $\Delta_0$-definable $P$, distinct variable indices $i \ne j$, a valuation $v$
with values in $L_\theta$ and $a \in L_\theta$,

$$
\bigl(\forall x \in a\ \exists y \in L_\theta\ P^{L_\theta}(v[i \mapsto x][j \mapsto y])\bigr)
\to
\exists b \in L_\theta\ \forall x \in a\ \exists y \in b\ P^{L_\theta}(v[i \mapsto x][j \mapsto y]).
$$

There is one claim to prove.

$$
\mathrm{Adm}(\theta) \iff \mathrm{KPModel}(\theta).
$$

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) $\Rightarrow$: the truth of the eight axioms from the three conditions

Split $\mathrm{KPAx}(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)$ into seven cases and show that each axiom is
true in $L_\theta$. Codes are injective, so an equality of codes yields an equality of
formulas. As for valuations, a lemma replacing only the free variables by values in $L_\theta$
normalizes the situation to the form "$v(n) \in L_\theta$ for every $n$" before use.

- **$\mathrm{Ext}$**: no condition on $\theta$ is needed. $L_\theta$ is transitive, so if
  $x,y \in L_\theta$ have the same elements then $x = y$ by extensionality.
- **$\mathrm{Emp}$**: if $\theta$ is a limit then $\emptyset \in L_\theta$.
- **$\mathrm{Pair}$**: if $x,y \in L_\theta$ then $\{x,y\} \in L_\theta$. Uses that $\theta$ is
  a limit.
- **$\mathrm{Un}$**: if $x \in L_\theta$ then $\bigcup x \in L_\theta$. Same.
- **$\mathrm{Inf}$**: take $\omega$ as the witness. $\omega \in L_\theta$ follows from
  $\omega < \theta$. Check the two inner conditions.
  - $\emptyset \in \omega$: $\bar 0 = \emptyset$, and $\forall v_2 \in \bar 0\ \bot$ is
    vacuously true.
  - closed under successor: for $\bar n \in \omega$, take
    $\overline{n+1} = \bar n \cup \{\bar n\}$ and check the three facts
    $\bar n \in \overline{n+1}$, $\bar n \subseteq \overline{n+1}$ and
    $\overline{n+1} \subseteq \bar n \cup \{\bar n\}$.
- **$\Delta_0$-Separation**: if $\theta$ is a limit, then for a $\Delta_0$ formula $\varphi$ and
  $x \in L_\theta$, the set $\{u \in x : \varphi^{L_\theta}[u]\}$ belongs to $L_\theta$. The
  only side condition needed is "the witness variable $y$ is not free in $\varphi$"; $j$ and
  $x$ are universally quantified in front, so they may occur freely as parameters.
- **$\Delta_0$-Collection**: exactly the third conjunct of $\mathrm{Adm}$.
- **Set Induction**: $\in$ is well-founded, so it holds for every formula.

#### (b) $\Leftarrow$: the three conditions from the truth of the eight axioms

**$\omega < \theta$.** From the truth of $\mathrm{Inf}$ take $x \in L_\theta$ with
$\emptyset \in x$ and $u \in x \to u \cup \{u\} \in x$. By induction on $n$, $\bar n \in x$ for
every $n$. Next show $n \le \mathrm{rank}(\bar n)$ by induction on $n$ (from
$\bar n \in \overline{n+1}$, $\mathrm{rank}(\bar n) < \mathrm{rank}(\overline{n+1})$). Since
$\bar n \in x$, $\mathrm{rank}(\bar n) < \mathrm{rank}(x)$, so $n \le \mathrm{rank}(x)$ for
every $n$, whence $\omega \le \mathrm{rank}(x)$. From $x \in L_\theta$,
$\mathrm{rank}(x) < \theta$. Hence $\omega < \theta$.

**$\theta$ is a limit.** From the truth of $\mathrm{Pair}$ and $\mathrm{Un}$, extract that
$L_\theta$ is closed under pairs and unions, and show that assuming $\theta$ to be a successor
gives a contradiction.

**$\Delta_0$-Collection.** This is the longest part. Suppose $\Delta_0$-definable $P$, $i \ne j$,
$v$ and $a$ are given. Take a $\Delta_0$ formula $\varphi$ representing $P$ and choose a
variable index $N$ larger than $i$, $j$, every variable of $\varphi$ and every index in the
support of $P$. Check that the code of the universal closure of
$\mathrm{Coll}(i,j,N,N+1,\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)$ is recognized by $\mathrm{KPAx}$. What is
needed is that $\varphi$ is $\Delta_0$, that $N$ is not free in $\varphi$, that $i \ne j$, and
that $i,j,N,N+1$ are distinct, all of which follow from the choice of $N$. Then replace $v$ by
a valuation $v'$ with values in $L_\theta$ (sending indices outside $s$ to $\emptyset$), use the
assumed truth with $v'$ to obtain a collection set $b \in L_\theta$, and finally return to the
conclusion about $P$ using that $v'$ and $v$ agree on $s \setminus \{i,j\}$.

#### (c) The existence of $\omega$ (the paper's (ii))

**No counterpart.** $\omega$ is given from the external universe. Lean's $\omega$ is the image
$\bar\omega = \{\bar 0,\bar 1,\dots\}$ of the external ordinal $\omega$, and its membership in
$L_\theta$ follows from $\omega < \theta$. The **derivation inside $\mathrm{KP}$** that the
paper describes — separating out the class of all finite ordinals from the witness of Infinity
by $\Delta_0$-Separation and proving minimality by Set Induction — is not formalized.

The argument deriving $\omega < \theta$ in (b) above is not a substitute for that derivation.
It only says, using the external $\mathrm{rank}$, that "if $L_\theta$ has an inductive set then
$\theta > \omega$"; it does not build $\omega$ inside $\mathrm{KP}$.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) The whole of §10 is $\models$ rather than $\vdash$

Section 10 of the paper is a sequence of syntactic claims of the form "$\mathrm{KP}$ proves the
following". Lean substitutes the semantic claim "if $\theta$ is admissible then it holds inside
$L_\theta$". Writing $\mathrm{KP} \vdash \psi$ would require

1. a proof system for first-order logic over the $\{\in\}$ language (an inductive type of
   derivations)
2. structural induction over derivations and a substitution lemma
3. rewriting each proof of §10 as an internal derivation of the object theory

The tree has no derivability relation at all and no completeness theorem, so there is no
shortcut from $\models$ to $\vdash$ either. Items 1 and 2 would take 1,500 to 2,500 lines, and
item 3 more than 3,000.

The substitute works because there are in fact only two places where the paper uses the results
of §10: inside $L_\theta$ for admissible $\theta$, and the external universe $V$
([P-14](P-14.md)). They are proved separately in these two models without going through a
soundness theorem. The price is that the same content is proved twice, and the generality
"holds in any model of $\mathrm{KP}$" is not obtained.

There is one intermediate stage. Generalizing §10 from "$L_\theta$ ($\theta$ admissible)" to
"any transitive model of $\mathrm{KP}$" would not be $\vdash$, but it would come closer to
"$\mathrm{KP}$ proves …". However, the current code depends throughout on closure lemmas
specific to the constructible hierarchy, so it would be a complete rewrite of more than 5,000
lines. In an abstract model, moreover, auxiliary sets cannot be built out of hierarchy stages,
so the full version of [P-07](P-07.md) (Lemma 10.4(3) by the paper's transitive-closure
recursion) would be a prerequisite.

### (ii) There is no internal derivation of the existence of $\omega$

As (c) above says, $\omega$ is given from the external universe. The derivation inside
$\mathrm{KP}$ that the paper describes — separating out the class of all finite ordinals from
the witness of Infinity by $\Delta_0$-Separation and proving minimality by Set Induction — is
not formalized.

The derivation itself is correct, and "$x$ is a finite ordinal" can be written in $\Delta_0$
form as: $x$ transitive $\wedge$ $\forall y \in x$ ($y$ transitive) $\wedge$ ($x = \emptyset$
or $x$ is a successor) $\wedge$ $\forall y \in x$ ($y = \emptyset$ or $y$ is a successor), so a
literal formalization would be neither false nor unprovable. There is simply no framework for
stating it in the form "$\mathrm{KP}$ proves it". The same root as (i).
