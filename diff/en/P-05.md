# P-05: Corollary 10.2, functional $\Sigma_1$-Replacement

## The paper

### Statement

Corollary 10.2 (functional $\Sigma_1$-Replacement). $\mathrm{KP}$ proves the following. If
$\psi(x,y,\bar p) \in \Sigma_1$ and

$$
\forall x \in a\ \exists! y\ \psi(x,y,\bar p)
$$

then there is a function $F$ with $\mathrm{dom}(F) = a$ satisfying $\psi(x,F(x),\bar p)$.

We also quote Lemma 10.1, on which it rests.

Lemma 10.1 ($\Sigma_1$-Collection). $\mathrm{KP}$ proves $\Sigma_1$-Collection. That is, for
$\psi(x,y,\bar p) \in \Sigma_1$, if $\forall x \in a\ \exists y\ \psi(x,y,\bar p)$ then there
is a set $b$ with $\forall x \in a\ \exists y \in b\ \psi(x,y,\bar p)$.

### Proof

By the witness-pair collection of Lemma 10.1 we obtain a single set $B$ containing all
candidate values $y$ and the auxiliary witnesses. In $\mathrm{KP}$ the product $a \times B$ can
be built by collecting Kuratowski pairs with $\Delta_0$-Collection. On top of that,

$$F = \{\langle x,y\rangle \in a \times B : \psi(x,y,\bar p)\}$$

is separated out by Separation with a $\Delta_0$ condition in which the auxiliary witnesses of
$\psi$ are also bounded by $B$. By the existence and uniqueness hypothesis, $F$ is exactly a
function on $a$.

We also quote the proof of Lemma 10.1.

Write $\psi(x,y,\bar p)$ as $\exists z\, \delta(x,y,z,\bar p)$ with $\delta \in \Delta_0$.
Collect the witnesses $y,z$ into a Kuratowski pair $w = \langle y,z\rangle$. The
quantification that extracts $y$ and $z$ from $w = \{\{y\},\{y,z\}\}$ can be bounded by $w$ and
finitely many of its unions, so "$w$ is a witness pair for $x$" can be written as a $\Delta_0$
formula. By $\Delta_0$-Collection we obtain a set $c$ containing at least one witness pair for
each $x \in a$. Put $d = \bigcup\bigcup c$; then every $y,z$ that was used belongs to $d$. Then

$$b = \{y \in d : \exists x \in a\ \exists w \in c\ \exists z \in d\ (w = \langle y,z\rangle \wedge \delta(x,y,z,\bar p))\}$$

is separated out by $\Delta_0$-Separation. For each $x \in a$ the first component of the
witness pair collected into $c$ belongs to $b$, so this is the required Collection set.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

**There is no theorem corresponding to Corollary 10.2.** No theorem asserting the existence of
the function $F$ exists in the tree.

There is a counterpart to Lemma 10.1. It handles the witnesses not one at a time but as a
**block** $l = [i_1,\dots,i_n]$. That the values can be taken in a domain $D$ is written

$$
\mathrm{Exs}_D([\,],P,v) :\Longleftrightarrow P(v), \qquad
\mathrm{Exs}_D(i :: l,P,v) :\Longleftrightarrow \exists y\ \bigl(D(y) \wedge \mathrm{Exs}_D(l,P,v[i \mapsto y])\bigr)
$$

and that they can be taken inside a set $b$ is written

$$
\mathrm{ExsIn}_b([\,],P,v) :\Longleftrightarrow P(v), \qquad
\mathrm{ExsIn}_b(i :: l,P,v) :\Longleftrightarrow \exists y \in b\ \mathrm{ExsIn}_b(l,P,v[i \mapsto y]).
$$

The Lean version of Lemma 10.1 is the following. Let $\theta$ be admissible, $Q$ a
$\Delta_0$-definable predicate, $l$ the block of witness variables, $i$ the element variable
and $a$ the range variable ($i \ne a$ and $i,a \notin l$).

$$
\bigl(\forall x \in v(a)\ \mathrm{Exs}_{L_\theta}(l,\,Q^{L_\theta},\,v[i \mapsto x])\bigr)
\ \Longrightarrow\
\exists b \in L_\theta\ \forall x \in v(a)\ \mathrm{ExsIn}_b(l,\,Q^{L_\theta},\,v[i \mapsto x]).
$$

The paper's form $\psi \in \Sigma_1$, $\psi = \exists z\,\delta$ is replaced by "a $\Delta_0$
matrix $Q$ and a witness block $l$". When $l$ is a single variable it is the paper's
$\exists z$.

### The proof in Lean

We write out the proof of Lemma 10.1. The paper collects the witnesses $y,z$ into a Kuratowski
pair $w = \langle y,z\rangle$ and takes $d = \bigcup\bigcup c$, whereas Lean collects the
witnesses of the whole block into a **finite set**, so one $\bigcup$ suffices.

**Step 1: take a fresh variable.** Take $c$ larger than every index in the support $s$ of $Q$,
than $i$, than $a$ and than the block $l$. Then $c \notin s$, $c \notin l$ and $c \ne i$.

**Step 2: normalize the valuation.** The values of the block variables are overwritten in the
end, so put $v'(k) := \emptyset$ for $k \in l$ and $v'(k) := v(k)$ otherwise. Since
$\emptyset \in L_\theta$ ($\theta$ a limit), $v'$ takes values in $L_\theta$ on
$s \setminus l$. Take a $\Delta_0$ formula $\varphi$ representing $Q$ and replace $Q$ by the
satisfaction $Q'$ of $\varphi$. Since $v$ and $v'$ agree on $s \setminus l$ and $\mathrm{Exs}$
overwrites the block variables, the hypothesis holds for $v'$ as well.

**Step 3: pack the witnesses for each $x$ into a single set.** Fix $x \in v(a)$. The
hypothesis gives $\mathrm{Exs}_{L_\theta}(l,Q',v'[i \mapsto x])$, that is, $Q'$ holds when each
variable of $l$ is given a value in $L_\theta$. Let $ys$ be the list of those values and take
$\mathrm{ofList}(ys)$ (the finite set collecting exactly the elements of $ys$). Since $\theta$
is a limit, $\mathrm{ofList}(ys) \in L_\theta$ and

$$
\mathrm{ExsIn}_{\mathrm{ofList}(ys)}(l,\,Q',\,v'[i \mapsto x])
$$

holds. Read this as "taking the value of $c$ to be $\mathrm{ofList}(ys)$ makes
$\mathrm{ExsIn}_{v(c)}(l,Q',v)$ hold".

**Step 4: apply $\Delta_0$-Collection.** The predicate

$$
R(v)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \mathrm{ExsIn}_{v(c)}(l,\,Q^{D},\,v)
$$

is $\Delta_0$. Indeed, by induction on the length of $l$: for $l = [\,]$ it is $Q$ itself, and
for $l = i :: l'$ it merely puts $\exists y \in v(c)$ on the induction hypothesis, so only
bounded quantifiers are added. The support is $\mathrm{insert}\ c\ s$.

Step 3 gives "$\forall x \in v(a)\ \exists y \in L_\theta\ R(v'[i \mapsto x][c \mapsto y])$",
so applying the third condition of admissibility ($\Delta_0$-Collection) to the variables
$i,c$ yields

$$
\exists b_0 \in L_\theta\ \forall x \in v(a)\ \exists w \in b_0\
\mathrm{ExsIn}_w(l,\,Q',\,v'[i \mapsto x]).
$$

**Step 5: take the union.** Put $b := \bigcup b_0$. Since $\theta$ is a limit,
$b \in L_\theta$. For each $x \in v(a)$ the $w$ of Step 4 satisfies
$w \subseteq \bigcup b_0 = b$, so monotonicity of $\mathrm{ExsIn}$,

$$
w \subseteq b \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{ExsIn}_w(l,P,v) \to \mathrm{ExsIn}_b(l,P,v),
$$

gives $\mathrm{ExsIn}_b(l,Q',v'[i \mapsto x])$.

**Step 6: restore the valuation.** The elements of $b$ belong to $L_\theta$ (by transitivity
of $L_\theta$, used twice). Restore $Q'$ to $Q$ and $v'$ to $v$ to obtain the conclusion.

The correspondence with the paper is as follows. The paper's $c$ is the $b_0$ of Step 4, and
the paper's $d = \bigcup\bigcup c$ is the $b = \bigcup b_0$ of Step 5. There is one $\bigcup$
fewer because the paper packs the witnesses into a Kuratowski pair
$\langle y,z\rangle = \{\{y\},\{y,z\}\}$ (which goes two levels deeper), whereas Lean packs the
witnesses of the block into the flat finite set $\mathrm{ofList}(ys)$. The $\Delta_0$-Separation
by which the paper finally cuts $b$ out is not needed in Lean: $\mathrm{ExsIn}_b$ states
directly that "there is a witness inside $b$", so there is no need to shrink $b$ down to the
witnesses.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There is one difference: there is no theorem corresponding to Corollary 10.2.

There is no framework for stating, as a theorem of $\mathrm{KP}$,

$$
\mathrm{KP} \vdash\ \bigl(\forall x \in a\ \exists! y\ \psi\bigr) \to
\exists F\ \bigl(\mathrm{dom}(F) = a \wedge \forall x \in a\ \psi(x,F(x),\bar p)\bigr).
$$

This is the same root as (i) of [P-04](P-04.md).

The semantic version, "if $\theta$ is admissible and the hypothesis holds in $L_\theta$ then
there is $F \in L_\theta$", can be written. Take $b \in L_\theta$ from the Lean version of
Lemma 10.1 and separate out $F = \{\langle x,y\rangle \in a \times b : \psi\}$ by
$\Delta_0$-Separation. The existence of $a \times b$ (collecting Kuratowski pairs with
$\Delta_0$-Collection) would take 80 to 120 lines, and the separation and the check that it is
a function about 60 more.

It is absent because at the four places where the paper uses Corollary 10.2 (the table above)
Lean takes a different route, and nothing downstream asks for it.
