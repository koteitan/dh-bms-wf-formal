# P-07: Lemma 10.4, finite codes and closure inside $\mathrm{KP}$

## The paper

### Statement

Lemma 10.4 (finite codes and closure inside $\mathrm{KP}$). $\mathrm{KP}$ proves the following.

1. For every $A$ the set $A^{<\omega}$ of all standard finite function codes exists.
2. For every $X$ the least transitive set $\mathrm{TC}(X)$ containing $X$ exists.
3. For every $X$ there is a set $U$ with $X \subseteq U$ such that $U$ is transitive and
   satisfies $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$.

### Proof

(1) Let $Q_0 = \{\emptyset\}$. Given that $Q_n$ exists, send each $(a,x)$ of $Q_n \times A$ to
the unique output $b$ of the append operation $\mathrm{Append}(a,x,b)$. The relation is
$\Delta_0$, so by Corollary 10.2 the range $Q_{n+1}$ exists. The $\omega$-recursion of Lemma
10.3 gives the sequence $(Q_n)_{n<\omega}$ as a single set. By induction on the length, $Q_n$
is exactly the set of all $A$-valued sequences of length $n$, and

$$A^{<\omega} = \bigcup_{n<\omega} Q_n$$

is a set.

(2) Recurse with $R_0 = X$ and $R_{n+1} = R_n \cup \bigcup R_n$ along $\omega$ and put
$T = \bigcup_{n<\omega} R_n$. If $y \in z \in T$ then $z \in R_n$ for some $n$, hence
$y \in R_{n+1} \subseteq T$, so $T$ is transitive. For any transitive set $Y$ containing $X$,
one shows $R_n \subseteq Y$ inductively, hence $T \subseteq Y$. Therefore
$T = \mathrm{TC}(X)$.

(3) Let $U_0 = \mathrm{TC}(X)$ and recurse with

$$U_{n+1} = \mathrm{TC}\bigl(U_n \cup \mathrm{Pair}(U_n) \cup \mathrm{Un}(U_n)\bigr).$$

$\mathrm{Pair}$, $\mathrm{Un}$, finite unions and $\mathrm{TC}$ are all unique $\Sigma_1$ set
operations, so Lemma 10.3 applies. Put $U = \bigcup_n U_n$. Each $U_n$ is transitive and they
increase, so $U$ is transitive and $X \subseteq U$. Any $x,y \in U$ lie in one and the same
$U_n$ for $n$ large enough, and $\{x,y\}, \bigcup x \in U_{n+1}$. Hence $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

All three are put in the semantic form "$\theta$ admissible, $X \in L_\theta$".

**(1)** The set of all finite sequences:

$$
\mathrm{Seqs}(A) := \{a \in \mathcal{P}(\omega \times A) : a \text{ is an } A \text{-valued standard finite sequence}\}
$$

$$
\theta \text{ admissible},\ A \in L_\theta \ \Longrightarrow\ \mathrm{Seqs}(A) \in L_\theta.
$$

**(2)** The transitive closure:

$$
\theta \text{ admissible},\ X \in L_\theta \ \Longrightarrow\
\exists T \in L_\theta\ \bigl(X \subseteq T \wedge \mathrm{Trans}(T) \wedge
\forall Y\ (X \subseteq Y \wedge \mathrm{Trans}(Y) \to T \subseteq Y)\bigr).
$$

Minimality is included, so this is exactly the paper's statement about $\mathrm{TC}(X)$.

**(3)** A transitive superset closed under pairs and unions:

$$
\theta \text{ admissible},\ X \in L_\theta \ \Longrightarrow\
\exists U \in L_\theta\ \bigl(X \subseteq U \wedge \mathrm{Trans}(U) \wedge \mathrm{PUCl}(U)\bigr).
$$

### The proof in Lean

#### (1) The set of all finite sequences

The paper builds $Q_n$ by $\omega$-recursion and takes the union, whereas Lean **builds it in
one step with the power set and Separation**.

$$
\mathrm{Seqs}(A) := \mathrm{sep}\bigl(a \mapsto a \text{ is an } A \text{-valued standard finite sequence},\
\mathcal{P}(\omega \times A)\bigr)
$$

Here $\omega \times A$ is built by Separation on pairs. If $a$ is an $A$-valued standard finite
sequence then $a \subseteq \omega \times A$, so $a \in \mathcal{P}(\omega \times A)$, and the
converse comes from the condition of the Separation. Hence the extension agrees with the
paper's $A^{<\omega}$.

The body of the work is showing $\mathrm{Seqs}(A) \in L_\theta$. The power set sits badly with
the $L$ hierarchy, so it is done in stages.

- $\mathrm{PowSeq}(A,n) := \{a \in \mathrm{Seqs}(A) : \mathrm{Dom}(a) = \bar n\}$ (all
  sequences of length exactly $n$). Show $\mathrm{PowSeq}(A,n) \in L_\theta$ by external
  induction on $n$. For $n = 0$ it is $\{\emptyset\}$. For $n+1$, take the image of the
  operation building $a \cup \{\langle\bar n,x\rangle\}$ from a sequence $a$ of length $n$ and
  $x \in A$.
- $\mathrm{SeqsLe}(A,\bar k) := \{a \in \mathrm{Seqs}(A) : \mathrm{Dom}(a) \subseteq \bar k\}$
  (length $\le k$), again by external induction on $k$.
- Finally, bundle the $\mathrm{SeqsLe}(A,\bar k)$ over $\omega$ with
  $\Sigma_1$-Collection and take the union to get $\mathrm{Seqs}(A)$.

Corollary 10.2 (existence of the range) and the $\omega$-recursion of Lemma 10.3, both used by
the paper, are replaced by **external induction on $\mathbb{N}$**.

#### (2) The transitive closure

The stages corresponding to the paper's $R_n$ are defined by external recursion.

$$
\mathrm{tc}_0(X) := X, \qquad \mathrm{tc}_{n+1}(X) := \mathrm{tc}_n(X) \cup \bigcup \mathrm{tc}_n(X).
$$

Since $\theta$ is a limit, each stage belongs to $L_\theta$. So far this is the same as the
paper. The problem is **building the union $\bigcup_n \mathrm{tc}_n(X)$ inside $L_\theta$**, and
that is where they differ. The paper obtains the sequence itself as a single set by
$\omega$-recursion, whereas Lean takes the following four steps.

*Step A: write the step relation in $\Delta_0$ form.*

$$
\mathrm{TCStep}(x,y)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ y = x \cup \bigcup x
$$

is equivalent to the following conjunction of three, which is $\Delta_0$.

$$
(\forall z \in y\ (z \in x \vee \exists u \in x\ z \in u)) \wedge
(\forall z \in x\ z \in y) \wedge
(\forall u \in x\ \forall z \in u\ z \in y).
$$

*Step B: build finite chains.* Define the chain of length $n+1$ and its range

$$
\mathrm{tcF}_n(X) := \{\langle \bar m, \mathrm{tc}_m(X)\rangle : m \le n\}, \qquad
\mathrm{tcR}_n(X) := \{\mathrm{tc}_m(X) : m \le n\}
$$

by external recursion. They are iterated $\mathrm{insert}$s, so they belong to $L_\theta$.

*Step C: bundle the chains with $\Sigma_1$-Collection.* The predicate

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Chain}(n,Y,R)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Fun}(Y) \wedge \mathrm{Ran}(Y) = R \wedge \langle \emptyset, X\rangle \in Y \\
&\wedge\ \exists d \in \omega\ \bigl(\mathrm{Dom}(Y) = d \wedge n \in d\ \wedge \\
&\qquad \forall m \in d\ \forall s \in d\ \forall w \in R\ \forall w' \in R\
 ((s = m^+ \wedge \langle m,w\rangle \in Y) \wedge \langle s,w'\rangle \in Y \to \mathrm{TCStep}(w,w'))\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

is $\Delta_0$ ($m,s$ are bounded by $d$ and $w,w'$ by $R$). For each $n \in \omega$,
$\mathrm{Chain}(n,\mathrm{tcF}_n(X),\mathrm{tcR}_n(X))$ holds, so applying
$\Sigma_1$-Collection with $n \in \omega$ and the witness block $(Y,R)$ yields
$b \in L_\theta$.

One lemma is inserted here. If $\mathrm{Chain}(n,Y,R)$ and $\mathrm{Dom}(Y) = \bar l$, then
$\langle\bar m,\mathrm{tc}_m(X)\rangle \in Y$ at every stage $m < l$. This follows by strong
induction on $m$ from functionality of $Y$ and uniqueness in $\mathrm{TCStep}$ ($y$ is
determined by $x$). It tells us that "the values of the chains inside $b$ are the stages
themselves".

*Step D: separate out $\mathrm{TC}$.* Put $V := \bigcup\bigcup\bigcup b$; by the lemma of Step
C, every stage $\mathrm{tc}_k(X)$ belongs to $V$. Hence
$\bigcup V \supseteq \bigcup_k \mathrm{tc}_k(X)$. So build

$$
T := \{z \in \textstyle\bigcup V : \exists k\ z \in \mathrm{tc}_k(X)\}
$$

by Separation. But "$\exists k$" is a meta-level quantifier and is not $\Delta_0$ as it stands.
Using the lemma of Step C, rewrite it as the $\Delta_0$ condition

$$
\exists Y \in b\ \exists R \in b\ \bigl(\text{the conditions of } \mathrm{Chain} \wedge \exists w \in R\ z \in w\bigr).
$$

This equivalence is the heart of Step D. $\Rightarrow$: the values of a chain are the stages,
so $z \in w = \mathrm{tc}_m(X)$. $\Leftarrow$: if $z \in \mathrm{tc}_k(X)$, take from $b$ a
chain containing $k$. Separation gives $T \in L_\theta$.

*Conclusion.* $X \subseteq T$ from $X = \mathrm{tc}_0(X)$. Transitivity: if $w \in T$ and
$z \in w$, then $w \in \mathrm{tc}_k(X)$ and $z \in \mathrm{tc}_{k+1}(X)$. Minimality: for
transitive $Y$ with $X \subseteq Y$, show $\mathrm{tc}_n(X) \subseteq Y$ by external induction
on $n$ (the $n+1$ stage uses transitivity of $Y$).

The paper's proof of (2) is four lines, whereas Lean needs three stages — a $\Delta_0$
description, $\Sigma_1$-Collection and Separation — to do the same thing, because
$\omega$-recursion is not available.

#### (3) A transitive superset closed under pairs and unions

**This is completely different from the paper.** The paper builds $U$ by a second
$\omega$-recursion,
$U_{n+1} = \mathrm{TC}(U_n \cup \mathrm{Pair}(U_n) \cup \mathrm{Un}(U_n))$. Lean takes a stage
of the hierarchy.

From $X \in L_\theta$ take $\zeta < \theta$ with $X \in L_\zeta$. Since $\theta$ is a limit,
there is a limit ordinal $\xi$ with $\zeta \le \xi < \theta$. Put $U := L_\xi$.

- $X \subseteq U$: from $X \in L_\zeta \subseteq L_\xi$ and transitivity of $L_\xi$.
- $\mathrm{Trans}(U)$: every stage of the $L$ hierarchy is transitive.
- $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$: if $x,y \in L_\xi$ and $\xi$ is a limit then $\{x,y\} \in L_\xi$ and
  $\bigcup x \in L_\xi$.

It takes three lines, but it uses neither $\mathrm{TC}$ nor $\mathrm{Pair}$ nor $\mathrm{Un}$.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) The line of the proof of 10.4(3)

The paper builds $U$ by the second $\omega$-recursion

$$
U_0 = \mathrm{TC}(X), \qquad
U_{n+1} = \mathrm{TC}\bigl(U_n \cup \mathrm{Pair}(U_n) \cup \mathrm{Un}(U_n)\bigr), \qquad
U = \bigcup_n U_n,
$$

whereas Lean takes $U := L_\xi$ ($\xi$ a limit above the rank of $X$). $\mathrm{TC}$ exists as
an independent theorem but is not used in the proof of 10.4(3). The statements agree.

Replacing this by the paper's route would require

- an image construction building $\mathrm{Pair}(U) = \{\{x,y\} : x,y \in U\}$ and
  $\mathrm{Un}(U) = \{\bigcup x : x \in U\}$ as sets (boundedness of the image + Separation on
  pairs + $\Delta_0$-Separation), 80 to 120 lines each
- a $\Delta_0$ description of the second chain with those stages, plus $\Sigma_1$-Collection,
  about 270 lines
- reproving transitivity, $\mathrm{PUCl}$ and $X \subseteq U$ for $U$, about 60 lines

That is +400 lines in total. The lemma that takes a stage of the hierarchy is used in three
other places as well, so the existing code would not shrink by the replacement.

### (ii) The $\omega$-recursions in (1) and (2)

The paper obtains both $(Q_n)$ and $(R_n)$ by the $\omega$-recursion of Lemma 10.3, whereas
Lean builds the stages by external induction on $\mathbb{N}$ in both cases and bundles them
with $\Sigma_1$-Collection. This is the same as (ii) of [P-06](P-06.md).
