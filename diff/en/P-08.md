# P-08: Lemma 10.5, satisfaction codes and $L$-codes inside $\mathrm{KP}$

## The paper

### Statement

Lemma 10.5 (satisfaction codes and $L$-codes inside $\mathrm{KP}$). $\mathrm{KP}$ proves the
following.

1. For every set $A$ there are $U,T$ satisfying $\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$. The truth part $T$
   is unique.
2. For every ordinal $\eta$ there are $M,c$ satisfying $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$.

### Proof

(1) By Lemma 10.4(1), $A^{<\omega}$ and $\omega \times A^{<\omega}$ exist. On top of that,
separate out by $\Delta_0$-Separation the set

$$W_A^{\mathrm{pr}} = \{\langle e,a\rangle \in \omega \times A^{<\omega} : e \text{ is a well-formedness code} \wedge \mathrm{Asn}_A(e,a)\}$$

consisting only of well-formedness codes and appropriate assignments. Recurse along the
syntactic depth on the cumulative sets $T_n \subseteq W_A^{\mathrm{pr}}$ collecting the correct
truth pairs of formulas of depth $< n$. The atomic stage is obtained by
$\Delta_0$-Separation. The negation, conjunction and existential stages are likewise uniquely
determined by $\Delta_0$-Separation, using the truth sets already obtained together with $A$
and $A^{<\omega}$ as bounding domains. Collect the sequence of stages by the
$\omega$-recursion of Lemma 10.3 and put $T = \bigcup_n T_n$. By construction
$T \subseteq W_A^{\mathrm{pr}}$.

Apply Lemma 10.4(3) to $\{A,\omega,T\}$ and take a common auxiliary set $U$ that is transitive
and satisfies $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$. The finite induction of Lemma 8.2 can be carried out inside
$\mathrm{KP}$ as well, so every appropriate $A$-valued assignment and every updated assignment
belongs to $U$. Hence $\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$ holds. The agreement of two truth parts can
also be proved inside $\mathrm{KP}$, by combining induction on syntactic depth for appropriate
pairs with the exclusion of inappropriate pairs by the second condition.

(2) For any set $X$, take the unique truth part $T_X$ from (1). By Lemma 10.4(1), the set of
definition inputs

$$I_X = \{\langle e,a\rangle \in \omega \times X^{<\omega} : \mathrm{DefInp}_X(e,a)\}$$

exists. For each $\langle e,a\rangle \in I_X$,

$$b_{e,a} = \{x \in X : \langle e,a_x\rangle \in T_X\}$$

exists uniquely by $\Delta_0$-Separation. Collecting the enumerating function $D_X$ for all
inputs by Corollary 10.2 gives $\mathrm{ran}(D_X) = \mathrm{Def}(X)$. By uniqueness of the
truth part, $Y = \mathrm{Def}(X)$ can be expressed as a unique $\Sigma_1$ set function.

Using Lemma 10.3, recurse along $\eta+1$ with

$$H(0) = \emptyset, \quad H(\xi+1) = \mathrm{Def}(H(\xi)), \quad H(\lambda) = \bigcup_{\xi<\lambda} H(\xi).$$

For each $\xi < \eta$, specify the unique truth part $S(\xi)$ of $H(\xi)$ by the $\Sigma_1$
relation "together with some auxiliary set it forms a satisfaction code", and collect them by
Corollary 10.2 into a single function $S$ on $\eta$. Likewise collect the exact enumeration of
definable subsets at each stage into $D$. Put $M = H(\eta)$.

Finally apply Lemma 10.4(3) to $\{\eta,M,H,S,D,\omega\}$ and take one $U$ that contains all the
data and is transitive and closed under Pairing and Union. From $H,S,D \in U$ and transitivity,
every function value belongs to $U$, and by Lemma 8.2 every $H(\xi)$-valued finite assignment
and every updated or concatenated assignment belongs to $U$ as well. Since the truth part
$S(\xi)$ is defined on all standard assignments, it satisfies
$\mathrm{SatCode}(H(\xi),U,S(\xi))$ together with this large common $U$. Finally, build the
function $c$ with domain $4$ by $c(0)=U, c(1)=H, c(2)=S, c(3)=D$. By Pairing and Union this
finite function code exists inside $\mathrm{KP}$ and $\mathrm{FourCode}(c,U,H,S,D)$ holds.
Hence $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

**(1)**

$$
\theta \text{ admissible},\ A \in L_\theta \ \Longrightarrow\
\exists U \in L_\theta\ \exists T \in L_\theta\ \mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T),
$$

$$
\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T) \wedge \mathrm{SatCode}(A,U',T') \ \Longrightarrow\ T = T'.
$$

Uniqueness is a claim about the external universe, depending on neither $\theta$ nor
$L_\theta$.

**(2)**

$$
\theta \text{ admissible},\ \xi < \theta \ \Longrightarrow\
\exists M \in L_\theta\ \exists c \in L_\theta\ \mathrm{LCode}(\xi,M,c).
$$

### The proof in Lean

#### (1-a) Defining the truth set

The paper recurses on $T_n$ along the syntactic depth and puts $T = \bigcup_n T_n$. Lean builds
it by **external recursion on the meta-level formula $\varphi$** and finally bundles the
results with $\Sigma_1$-Collection.

First, put the set of truth pairs for a single formula $\varphi$ to be

$$
\mathrm{TAtom}(A,\varphi) := \{\langle \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,a\rangle :
a \text{ an } A \text{-valued assignment covering } \mathrm{fv}(\varphi),\ (A,\in) \models \varphi[a]\}.
$$

This can be built by Separation from $\{\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner\} \times \mathrm{Seqs}(A)$.
Next, define the truth pairs of $\varphi$ and all its subformulas by **structural induction** on
$\varphi$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{TSet}(A,\bot) &= \mathrm{TAtom}(A,\bot), \\
\mathrm{TSet}(A, v_i = v_j) &= \mathrm{TAtom}(A, v_i = v_j), \\
\mathrm{TSet}(A, v_i \in v_j) &= \mathrm{TAtom}(A, v_i \in v_j), \\
\mathrm{TSet}(A, \varphi \to \psi) &= \mathrm{TAtom}(A,\varphi\to\psi) \cup \mathrm{TSet}(A,\varphi) \cup \mathrm{TSet}(A,\psi), \\
\mathrm{TSet}(A, \forall v_i\,\varphi) &= \mathrm{TAtom}(A,\forall v_i\,\varphi) \cup \mathrm{TSet}(A,\varphi).
\end{aligned}
$$

Define the set $\mathrm{Cs}(\varphi)$ of subformula codes by structural induction likewise.
Both belong to $L_\theta$ when $\theta$ is a limit (structural induction on $\varphi$, closure
under $\cup$ and Separation).

**The truth set itself** is defined as

$$
\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) := \{z \in L_\omega \times \mathrm{Seqs}(A) :
\exists \varphi\ \exists a\ (z = \langle\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,a\rangle \wedge
a \text{ covers } \mathrm{fv}(\varphi) \wedge (A,\in)\models\varphi[a])\}.
$$

Since "$\exists\varphi$" is a meta-level quantifier, this Separation condition is **not**
$\Delta_0$. So $\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) \in L_\theta$ is proved another way.

#### (1-b) $\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) \in L_\theta$

Write the Tarski clauses as $\Delta_0$ predicates on codes. With $C$ a set of codes and $t$ a
set of truth pairs, put

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SubCl}(W,C_0,C)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ &\forall e \in C\ \bigl(
(\forall e_1,e_2 \in C_0\ (e = \langle\bar 3,\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\rangle \to e_1,e_2 \in C)) \\
&\qquad \wedge\ (\forall i \in W\ \forall e' \in C_0\ (e = \langle\bar 4,\langle i,e'\rangle\rangle \to e' \in C))\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

($C$ is closed under immediate subformula codes), and let
$\mathrm{TClause}(A,S,W,C_0,C,t)$ say "for every code in $C$ and every assignment in $S$, the
set $t$ satisfies the Tarski clauses". That is, no pair for $\bot$ is in $t$; the pairs for
$v_i = v_j$ and $v_i \in v_j$ are decided by the values inside $A$; and the pairs for $\to$ and
$\forall$ are determined by the membership of the pairs for the children in $t$. Both are
$\Delta_0$.

Lemma (correctness): if $\mathrm{SubCl}$ and $\mathrm{TClause}$ hold and
$\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner \in C$, then

$$
\langle\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,a\rangle \in t \iff (A,\in)\models\varphi[a].
$$

Shown by structural induction on $\varphi$.

Next use $\Sigma_1$-Collection. For $e \in L_\omega$, the predicate

$$
\exists c\ \exists t\ \bigl(\mathrm{SubCl}(\omega,L_\omega,c) \wedge
\mathrm{TClause}(A,\mathrm{Seqs}(A),\omega,L_\omega,c,t) \wedge (\mathrm{Form}(e) \to e \in c)\bigr)
$$

is a $\Delta_0$ matrix with the witness block $(c,t)$. Witnesses can be taken to be
$(\mathrm{Cs}(\varphi), \mathrm{TSet}(A,\varphi))$ when $e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$, and
$(\emptyset,\emptyset)$ when $e$ is not a code. Hence $\Sigma_1$-Collection yields
$b \in L_\theta$ containing a witness for each $e \in L_\omega$.

Finally, show that $\mathrm{TruthSet}(A)$ can be written by a **$\Delta_0$ Separation with $b$
as a parameter**:

$$
\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) = \{z \in L_\omega \times \mathrm{Seqs}(A) :
\exists c \in b\ \exists t \in b\ (\mathrm{SubCl} \wedge \mathrm{TClause} \wedge
\exists e \in L_\omega\ \exists a \in \mathrm{Seqs}(A)\ (z = \langle e,a\rangle \wedge
\mathrm{Form}(e) \wedge \mathrm{Asn}_A(e,a) \wedge e \in c \wedge z \in t))\}.
$$

$\supseteq$: for $z$ on the right, $\mathrm{Form}(e)$ gives $e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$, and
the correctness lemma above makes $z \in t$ mean $(A,\in)\models\varphi[a]$.
$\subseteq$: if $z = \langle\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,a\rangle$ is on the left, take from $b$ a
witness $(c,t)$ for $\varphi$ and use the correctness lemma in the other direction. The
condition on the right is $\Delta_0$, so the Separation is legitimate and
$\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) \in L_\theta$.

#### (1-c) Existence of the satisfaction code

Apply Lemma 10.4(3) ([P-07](P-07.md)) to
$X := \{A,\ \omega,\ \mathrm{TruthSet}(A)\}$ and take a transitive $U \in L_\theta$ with
$\mathrm{PUCl}$. This is the same as the paper's "apply Lemma 10.4(3) to $\{A,\omega,T\}$". If
$U$ is transitive with $A,\omega,\mathrm{TruthSet}(A) \in U$ and $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$, show that
$(A,U,\mathrm{TruthSet}(A))$ is a satisfaction code. Among the conditions of Definition 8.3,
that the assignments and the updated assignments belong to $U$ follows from Lemma 8.2 (a finite
sequence inside a $\mathrm{PUCl}$ set).

#### (1-d) Uniqueness

The paper argues "by induction on syntactic depth and exclusion of inappropriate pairs by the
second condition". Lean follows the same line but runs **structural induction on $\varphi$**
rather than induction on depth. Assume $\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$ and
$\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U',T')$ and let $z \in T$. The second condition of Definition 8.3 (the
elements of $T$ are only pairs of a code and an appropriate assignment) gives
$z = \langle e,a\rangle$ with $e$ a code and $a$ an appropriate assignment. Write
$e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$ and apply correctness of the satisfaction code (Lemma 8.4) on
both sides:

$$
z \in T \iff (A,\in)\models\varphi[a] \iff z \in T'.
$$

The other direction is the same, and extensionality gives $T = T'$.

#### (2) Existence of the $L$-code

The paper extracts $\mathrm{Def}$ as a $\Sigma_1$ set function, recurses $H$ by Lemma 10.3, and
collects $S$ and $D$ by Corollary 10.2. Lean **builds the hierarchy externally**.

Define the stage values

$$
\mathrm{LTr}(\zeta) := \mathrm{TruthSet}(L_\zeta), \qquad
\mathrm{LFn}(\zeta) := \langle L_\zeta,\ \mathrm{LTr}(\zeta)\rangle
$$

(using the external $L$ hierarchy as it stands). Show that the graph of $\mathrm{LFn}$ below
$\xi+1$ belongs to $L_\theta$ by the $\Sigma_1$-recursion of [P-06](P-06.md). The step relation
is "the first component of $\langle A,T\rangle$ is the set of all definable subsets of the
previous stage, and the second component is its truth part", and one has to check that this is
$\Sigma_1$ and that it is unique.

Next take a limit $\delta < \theta$ containing that graph and base everything on $L_\delta$.
Put $H := \mathrm{gr}(L,\xi+1)$, $S := \mathrm{gr}(\mathrm{LTr},\xi)$ and
$D := \mathrm{gr}(\zeta \mapsto \mathrm{DefEnum}(L_\zeta,\mathrm{LTr}(\zeta)),\xi)$. These
correspond to the paper's $H,S,D$, but what the paper "collects" by Corollary 10.2, Lean
already has as the graph of an external function.

Finally apply Lemma 10.4(3) to

$$
X := \{H,\ S,\ D,\ L_\delta\}
$$

to take $U \in L_\theta$, put
$c := \{\langle\bar 0,U\rangle,\langle\bar 1,H\rangle,\langle\bar 2,S\rangle,\langle\bar 3,D\rangle\}$
and check each condition of $\mathrm{LCode}(\xi,L_\xi,c)$. Condition 3 (each stage forms a
satisfaction code) applies (1-c) at each $\zeta \le \xi$. Condition 4 (enumeration of the
definable subsets) comes from the construction of $\mathrm{DefEnum}$. Condition 5 (successor
and limit) comes from the properties of the $L$ hierarchy.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are three differences.

### (i) The line of the construction of the truth set $T$

This is a difference in the proof, not in the statement.

| | The paper | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| starting point | separate out $W_A^{\mathrm{pr}}$ by $\Delta_0$-Separation | no counterpart |
| recursion | $T_n$ along the syntactic depth $n$ | $\mathrm{TSet}(A,\varphi)$ along the meta-level formula $\varphi$ |
| aggregation | $T = \bigcup_n T_n$ by the $\omega$-recursion of Lemma 10.3 | obtain $b$ by $\Sigma_1$-Collection on codes, then $\Delta_0$-Separation |

Reproducing the paper's route would require

- a $\Delta_0$ definition of the syntactic depth relation $\mathrm{Dep}(e,n)$ on codes. Since
  codes are hereditarily finite sets rather than natural numbers, the recursion on codes has to
  be written in the schema style of [P-02](P-02.md). About 300 lines
- the $\Delta_0$-Separation for
  $T_{n+1} = \{\langle e,a\rangle \in W_A^{\mathrm{pr}} : \mathrm{Dep}(e) \le n \wedge
  (\text{the Tarski clauses relative to } T_n)\}$. About 200 lines
- collecting $(T_n)$ by the $\omega$-recursion of [P-06](P-06.md) and putting
  $T = \bigcup_n T_n$. About 150 lines
- reproving the satisfaction code for the new $T$ and replacing three downstream files. About
  300 lines

That is 800 to 1,200 lines in total, and the theorem obtained is identical to the present one.
The truth set is used from several places in §10 and §11, so the replacement would amount to
reproving §10 and §11.

A partial step is possible. The set that the paper's proof introduces first,

$$
W_A^{\mathrm{pr}} = \{\langle e,a\rangle \in \omega \times A^{<\omega} :
e \text{ is a well-formedness code} \wedge \mathrm{Asn}_A(e,a)\},
$$

can be built in 30 to 50 lines with the existing tools alone (the set of all finite sequences,
Separation on pairs, the $\Delta_0$-ness of $\mathrm{Form}$ and $\mathrm{Asn}$, and Separation
inside $L_\theta$).

### (ii) $\models$ rather than $\vdash$

Instead of
$\mathrm{KP} \vdash \forall\eta\,(\mathrm{Ord}(\eta) \to \exists M\exists c\ \mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c))$,
it is "if $\theta$ is admissible and $\eta < \theta$ then $M,c$ exist inside $L_\theta$". This
is the same root as (i) of [P-04](P-04.md).

The line of the proof differs as well. The paper extracts $\mathrm{Def}$ as a $\Sigma_1$ set
function and collects $H,S,D$ by Corollary 10.2, whereas Lean uses the external $L$ hierarchy
as it stands and shows only that its graph belongs to $L_\theta$, by $\Sigma_1$-recursion. It
does not build the hierarchy inside $\mathrm{KP}$.

### (iii) The set to which 10.4(3) is applied

The paper says to apply Lemma 10.4(3) to $\{\eta,M,H,S,D,\omega\}$, whereas Lean applies it to
$\{H,S,D,L_\delta\}$. Since transitivity of $L_\delta$ makes $\eta$, $M$ and $\omega$ belong to
$U$, the conclusion is the same, but the set applied to reads differently. On the side of (1),
$\{A,\omega,T\}$ is as in the paper.
