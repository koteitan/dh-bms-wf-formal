# P-12: Definition 13.4, the partial truth predicate

## The paper

### Statement

Definition 13.4 (the partial truth predicate). For $q = 1$, using the fixed code projections
$\nu = \mathrm{Vars}(e)$ and $d = \mathrm{Body}(e)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_1}(e,a) &\iff \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_1}(e) \wedge
\exists t\,\exists b\ \bigl(\mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \wedge \mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(d,b)\bigr), \\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Pi_1}(e,a) &\iff \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Pi_1}(e) \wedge
\forall t\,\forall b\ \bigl(\mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \to \mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(d,b)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

For $q \ge 1$, writing $d$ again for the body code of a $\hat\Sigma_{q+1}$ or $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$
formula with its outermost block removed, inductively

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}(e,a) &\iff \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}(e) \wedge
\exists t\,\exists b\ \bigl(\mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \wedge \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Pi_q}(d,b)\bigr), \\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Pi_{q+1}}(e,a) &\iff \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Pi_{q+1}}(e) \wedge
\forall t\,\forall b\ \bigl(\mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \to \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_q}(d,b)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here, if $t$ is not a finite sequence of the same length as $\nu$, then $\mathrm{BlkUpd}$ is
false.

### Proof

It is a definition, so there is no proof. It presupposes the form of $\hat\Sigma_q$ from
Definition 12.1,

$$
\exists\vec x_1\,\forall\vec x_2 \cdots Q_q \vec x_q\ \delta, \qquad \delta \in \Delta_0
$$

(each $\vec x_i$ a nonempty finite block of distinct variables).

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

**The type of block formulas.** An inductive type corresponding to the $\hat\Sigma_q$ /
$\hat\Pi_q$ formulas of the paper is set up.

$$
b ::= \delta(sg,\varphi) \ \mid\ \exists\vec l\,b \ \mid\ \forall\vec l\,b
$$

Here $sg \in \{0,1\}$ is a **sign bit**, $\varphi$ is a $\Delta_0$ formula, and $\vec l$ is a
finite list of variable indices. The corresponding formula is

$$
\|\delta(1,\varphi)\| = \varphi, \quad \|\delta(0,\varphi)\| = \neg\varphi, \quad
\|\exists\vec l\,b\| = \exists\vec l\,\|b\|, \quad \|\forall\vec l\,b\| = \forall\vec l\,\|b\|.
$$

The codes are

$$
\ulcorner\delta(sg,\varphi)\urcorner = \langle\bar 0,\langle \overline{sg},\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner\rangle\rangle, \quad
\ulcorner\exists\vec l\,b\urcorner = \langle\bar 1,\langle\ulcorner\vec l\urcorner,\ulcorner b\urcorner\rangle\rangle, \quad
\ulcorner\forall\vec l\,b\urcorner = \langle\bar 2,\langle\ulcorner\vec l\urcorner,\ulcorner b\urcorner\rangle\rangle.
$$

**Level $0$ (the signed $\Delta_0$ matrix).** This is a level with no counterpart in the paper.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}^{M}_{\Sigma}(e,a)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\Delta_0\mathrm{BFCode}(e) \wedge \exists sg \in D\ \exists d \in D\
\bigl(e = \langle\bar 0,\langle sg,d\rangle\rangle\ \wedge \\
&\quad \exists b \in D\ \bigl(b \text{ is the } \emptyset\text{-padding of } a \wedge
((sg = \bar 1 \wedge \mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(d,b)) \vee (sg = \bar 0 \wedge \neg\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(d,b)))\bigr)\bigr), \\
\mathrm{Tr}^{M}_{\Pi}(e,a)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\Delta_0\mathrm{BFCode}(e) \wedge \forall sg \in D\ \forall d \in D\
\bigl(e = \langle\bar 0,\langle sg,d\rangle\rangle \to \\
&\quad \forall b \in D\ (b \text{ is the } \emptyset\text{-padding of } a \to
((sg = \bar 1 \to \mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(d,b)) \wedge (sg = \bar 0 \to \neg\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(d,b))))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

**Level $q$.** Defined by mutual recursion on $q$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_0}(e,a) &= \mathrm{Tr}^{M}_{\Sigma}(e,a), \\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_1}(e,a) &= \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_1}(e) \wedge
\exists \nu \in D\ \exists d \in D\ \exists t \in D\ \exists b \in D\ \\
&\qquad \bigl(e = \langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,d\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \wedge \mathrm{Tr}^{M}_{\Sigma}(d,b)\bigr), \\
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+2}}(e,a) &= \mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+2}}(e) \wedge
\exists \nu \in D\ \exists d \in D\ \exists t \in D\ \exists b \in D\ \\
&\qquad \bigl(e = \langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,d\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b) \wedge \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Pi_{q+1}}(d,b)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

The $\hat\Pi$ side is obtained by turning every $\exists \nu \cdots$ into
$\forall \nu \cdots \to$ and the tag $\bar 1$ into $\bar 2$.

$\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_q}(e)$ includes, as in Definition 12.1, the condition that the
variables inside a block are distinct.

**Block update** $\mathrm{BlkUpd}(a,\nu,t,b)$ says that $\nu$ and $t$ are finite functions with
the same domain and that $b$ consists of pairs of the following three kinds.

- $\langle k,x\rangle$ such that for some $m$, $\langle m,k\rangle \in \nu$ and $\langle m,x\rangle \in t$ (the new values of the block variables)
- a pair of $a$ whose first component is not a value of $\nu$ (outside the block, unchanged)
- $\langle k,\emptyset\rangle$ where $k$ is not in the domain of $a$, is not a value of $\nu$, and is at most some value of $\nu$ (the $\emptyset$-padding)

The third kind is the part absent from the paper's $\mathrm{BlkUpd}$; it corresponds to
"$\mathrm{dom}(b) = m$, filling the remaining coordinates with $\emptyset$" just after
Definition 12.1.

### The proof in Lean

It is a definition, so there is no proof. For the two points where the shape of the definition
differs from the paper, we give the grounds for the agreement of the extensions.

#### (a) The extension of the form with $\mathrm{Vars}/\mathrm{Body}$ quantified

The paper writes $\nu = \mathrm{Vars}(e)$ and $d = \mathrm{Body}(e)$ as
$\Delta_0$-definable **function symbols** in term position, and the only quantifiers are
$\exists t\,\exists b$ (on the $\hat\Pi$ side $\forall t\,\forall b$). Lean cannot use function
symbols as terms, so $\nu$ and $d$ are quantified as well and the decomposition of the code is
written as the equation

$$
e = \langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,d\rangle\rangle.
$$

Two quantifiers are added and they are bounded by $D$.

The extensions agree for the following reason. The decomposition of a Kuratowski pair is
unique: if $\langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,d\rangle\rangle = \langle\bar 1,\langle\nu',d'\rangle\rangle$
then $\nu = \nu'$ and $d = d'$. Hence if $e$ is a $\hat\Sigma_{q+1}$ code, there is exactly one
pair $(\nu,d)$ satisfying the equation above, namely
$(\mathrm{Vars}(e),\mathrm{Body}(e))$.

As for the bounding by $D$: if $D$ is transitive and $e \in D$, then $\nu,d \in D$ is
automatic, so no condition is added. The $D$ actually used is $L_\theta$, or $V$, or a
transitive $M$, all of which are transitive.

There is no effect on the complexity either (Lemma 13.5, $\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_q} \in
\hat\Sigma_q$). The added $\exists\nu\,\exists d$ merge into the same leading block as the
existing $\exists t\,\exists b$, so the number of alternations does not change.

#### (b) The extension of the sign-bit layer

The base of the paper's Definition 13.4 is $q = 1$, and what appears there is
$\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(d,b)$ (on the $\hat\Pi$ side $\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(d,b)$). Here
$d$ is the code of a $\Delta_0$ formula and carries no sign.

Lean makes the base of the type of block formulas $\delta(sg,\varphi)$ (with a sign bit), so
the truth predicate at the base splits on the sign. For $sg = 1$ it is
$\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}$ and for $sg = 0$ it is $\neg\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}$ (on the
$\hat\Pi$ side, $\mathrm{Tr}^-$ for $sg=1$ and $\neg\mathrm{Tr}^+$ for $sg=0$).

The extensions agree because $\Delta_0$ is closed under negation. $\delta(0,\varphi)$ denotes
$\neg\varphi$, and $\neg\varphi$ is again a $\Delta_0$ formula, so in the paper's framework the
single $\Delta_0$ code $\ulcorner\neg\varphi\urcorner$ suffices. By Lemma 13.3 of
[P-11](P-11.md), $\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(\ulcorner\neg\varphi\urcorner,b)$ is equivalent to
$\neg\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,b)$, so the two ways of writing it have
the same expressive power.

Lean additionally has the level $q = 0$ ($\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_0} = \mathrm{Tr}^M_\Sigma$).
The paper's Definition 13.4 starts from $q \ge 1$, so this level has no direct counterpart.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) $\mathrm{Vars}/\mathrm{Body}$ are bounded quantifiers rather than terms

Lean has no framework for writing a $\Delta_0$-definable function in term position. The object
language is the pure $\{\in\}$ language with no function symbols, and a $\Delta_0$ formula has
the shape "give values to the variables of the support by a valuation", so there is
structurally no place to write the term $\mathrm{Vars}(e)$.

As (a) above shows, the extensions agree and the complexity is unaffected. Providing a
framework in which function symbols can be written in term position would first require
term-forming for $\Delta_0$-definable functions (introducing a function symbol from its graph
and eliminating the definitional extension), and that would mean rewriting the whole of §13.
The paper itself says in §8 that "the output quantification introduced when eliminating the
syntactic function symbols into the pure language is bounded by $\omega$ and does not increase
the Lévy complexity", so Lean can also be read as writing the already-eliminated form.

The same difference appears for $H(\xi), S(\xi), D(\xi)$ in [P-03](P-03.md).

### (ii) There is a sign-bit layer on the $\Delta_0$ matrix

The base of the paper's Definition 13.4 is $\mathrm{Tr}^\pm_{\Delta_0}(d,b)$, where $d$ is the
code of a $\Delta_0$ formula and carries no sign. Lean makes the base of the type of block
formulas $\delta(sg,\varphi)$ and splits the truth predicate at the base on the sign. It also
has the level $q = 0$, one level more than the paper's Definition 13.4 ($q \ge 1$), with no
direct counterpart.

As (b) above shows, the expressive power is unchanged because $\Delta_0$ is closed under
negation. To remove the sign bit one would need to

- change the base of the type of block formulas to $\delta(\varphi)$ (unsigned)
- change dualization into the form "put $\neg$ at the base"; that the negation of a $\Delta_0$
  formula is $\Delta_0$ is already available
- replace $\mathrm{Tr}^M_\Sigma,\mathrm{Tr}^M_\Pi$ by $\mathrm{Tr}^\pm_{\Delta_0}$ themselves
- follow through in the recognizer for $\Delta_0$ matrix codes on the sign side, in the graph
  of dualization (12.2(2) of [P-10](P-10.md)) and in the graph of padding

Downstream uses only the $\hat\Sigma_q$ shape and the complexity of the truth predicate, so the
ripple is expected to stay inside §12 and §13. There is no technical obstacle.
