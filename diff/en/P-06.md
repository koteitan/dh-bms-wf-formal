# P-06: Lemma 10.3, $\Sigma_1$-recursion on the ordinals in $\mathrm{KP}$

## The paper

### Statement

Lemma 10.3 ($\Sigma_1$-recursion on the ordinals in $\mathrm{KP}$). Let $\eta$ be an ordinal
and $\Phi(\xi,g,y,\bar p)$ a $\Sigma_1$ formula. If $\exists! y\ \Phi(\xi,g,y,\bar p)$ holds for
every $\xi \le \eta$ and every function $g$ on $\xi$ already satisfying the recursion equation,
then there is exactly one function $F$ with

$$
\mathrm{dom}(F) = \eta+1, \qquad
\Phi(\xi,\ F\!\restriction\!\xi,\ F(\xi),\ \bar p) \quad (\xi \le \eta).
$$

The $\omega$-recursion on natural number stages exists likewise.

### Proof

A partial approximation is a function $s$ whose domain is an ordinal $\lambda \le \eta+1$ and
which satisfies $\Phi(\xi, s\!\restriction\!\xi, s(\xi), \bar p)$ for every $\xi < \lambda$.
Since $\Phi$ is $\Sigma_1$, collecting the auxiliary witnesses for each $\xi \in \lambda$ into
one set by Lemma 10.1 makes "$s$ is a partial approximation" writable as $\Sigma_1$.

Two partial approximations $s,t$ agree on the common domain. If there were a least $\xi$ where
they disagree, then $s\!\restriction\!\xi = t\!\restriction\!\xi$, contradicting that $\Phi$
determines the value uniquely.

By Set Induction, show that a partial approximation with domain $\lambda$ exists for every
$\lambda \le \eta+1$. For $\lambda = 0$ it is the empty function. For $\lambda = \xi+1$, build
$s \cup \{\langle\xi,y\rangle\}$ from a partial approximation $s$ on $\xi$ and the unique next
value $y$.

If $\lambda$ is a limit, then for each $\xi < \lambda$ a partial approximation $s_\xi$ with
domain $\xi$ exists and, by the uniqueness above, is the only one. Since "$s$ is a partial
approximation with domain $\xi$" is $\Sigma_1$, Corollary 10.2 collects a function $G$ with
$G(\xi) = s_\xi$. By uniqueness the functions in $\mathrm{ran}(G)$ agree where they overlap, so
$s = \bigcup \mathrm{ran}(G)$ is a function. Since $\lambda$ is a limit, $\zeta+1 < \lambda$ for
every $\zeta < \lambda$, so $s_{\zeta+1}$ has a value at $\zeta$ and $\mathrm{dom}(s) = \lambda$.
Each recursion equation holds inside one sufficiently long partial approximation and therefore
holds for $s$ as well.

Taking $\lambda = \eta+1$ gives the required $F$. Uniqueness follows from the agreement of
partial approximations. For the $\omega$-recursion, extend the rule to $\omega+1$, fix the last
value, apply this result and then restrict to $\omega$.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

Let $\theta$ be an admissible ordinal and $\beta < \theta$ an upper bound (corresponding to the
paper's $\eta+1$). The $\Sigma_1$ formula $\Phi$ is given as a pair of "a $\Delta_0$ matrix $Q$
and a witness block $l$". The variables $0,1,2$ carry $\xi, g, y$, and $l$ is disjoint from
$\{0,1,2\}$.

**The step relation** (the paper's $\Phi(\xi,g,y,\bar p)$):

$$
\Phi_\theta(\xi,g,y)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
y \in L_\theta \ \wedge\ \mathrm{Exs}_{L_\theta}\bigl(l,\ Q^{L_\theta},\ v[0 \mapsto \xi][1 \mapsto g][2 \mapsto y]\bigr).
$$

$\mathrm{Exs}$ is the block existential quantification of [P-05](P-05.md).

**The graph below $\xi$** (the paper's $F\!\restriction\!\xi$):

$$
\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) := \{\langle \zeta, F(\zeta)\rangle : \zeta < \xi\}.
$$

**Partial approximation** (the paper's $s$, in a form making the set $b$ holding the witnesses
explicit):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{PA}(\zeta,g,b)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Fun}(g) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(g) = \zeta\ \wedge \\
&\forall \zeta' \in \zeta\ \exists g' \in b\ \exists y \in b\
\bigl(g' = g\!\restriction\!\zeta' \wedge \langle\zeta',y\rangle \in g \wedge
\mathrm{ExsIn}_b(l,\,Q,\,v[0\mapsto\zeta'][1\mapsto g'][2\mapsto y])\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Where the paper says "collecting the auxiliary witnesses into one set by Lemma 10.1 makes it
writable as $\Sigma_1$", Lean carries the collecting set $b$ as an argument from the start and
so makes it **$\Delta_0$**.

There are four claims to prove.

**(1) The graph belongs to $L_\theta$** (with an upper bound). For
$F : \mathrm{Ord} \to \mathrm{ZFSet}$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(\forall \xi < \beta\ (\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta \to \Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),F(\xi))\bigr)\ \wedge \\
&\bigl(\forall \xi < \beta\ (\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta \to \forall y \in L_\theta\ (\Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),y) \to y = F(\xi)))\bigr) \\
&\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \forall \xi \le \beta\ \ \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta.
\end{aligned}
$$

**(2) Unique existence** (the shape of the paper's statement). Under the same hypotheses,

$$
\exists! G\ \bigl(G \in L_\theta \wedge \mathrm{Fun}(G) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(G) = \beta \wedge
\forall \xi < \beta\ \Phi_\theta(\xi,\ G\!\restriction\!\xi,\ G(\xi))\bigr).
$$

Taking $\beta := \eta+1$ gives $\mathrm{dom}(G) = \eta+1$ and $\forall\xi \le \eta$, matching
the paper as printed.

**(3) The form that builds $F$.** With the hypothesis reduced to exactly the paper's ("for
every $\xi < \beta$ and every function $g \in L_\theta$ on $\xi$ satisfying the recursion
equation, $\exists! y\ \Phi_\theta(\xi,g,y)$"), obtain the same conclusion (2). Here $F$ is not
a hypothesis but an object produced in the conclusion.

**(4) $\omega$-recursion.** (3) with $\beta := \omega$. That $\omega < \theta$ is the first
condition of admissibility.

$$
\bigl(\forall \xi < \omega\ \cdots \exists! y\ \Phi_\theta(\xi,g,y)\bigr)
\Longrightarrow
\exists! G\ \bigl(G \in L_\theta \wedge \mathrm{Fun}(G) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(G) = \omega \wedge \forall\xi<\omega\ \Phi_\theta(\xi,G\!\restriction\!\xi,G(\xi))\bigr).
$$

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) Uniqueness of partial approximations

This corresponds to the paper's "two partial approximations agree on the common domain". Lean
shows it with one of them fixed to $\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi)$.

$$
\forall \xi < \beta\ \forall g\,b\ \bigl((\forall z \in b\ z \in L_\theta) \wedge \mathrm{PA}(\xi,g,b) \to g = \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi)\bigr)
$$

By well-founded induction on $\xi$. From $\mathrm{PA}(\xi,g,b)$ it follows that for each
$\zeta' < \xi$ the restriction $g\!\restriction\!\zeta'$ is a partial approximation on $\zeta'$
(with witnesses again inside $b$), so the induction hypothesis gives
$g\!\restriction\!\zeta' = \mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta')$. Then the third conjunct of $\mathrm{PA}$
gives $\Phi_\theta(\zeta',\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta'),g(\zeta'))$, so uniqueness in the hypothesis
gives $g(\zeta') = F(\zeta')$. Together with $\mathrm{dom}(g) = \xi$ this gives
$g = \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi)$.

Where the paper runs Set Induction, Lean runs well-founded induction on the external ordinals.

#### (b) Claim (1): transfinite induction on $\xi$

Where the paper shows "a partial approximation exists for every $\lambda \le \eta+1$", Lean
shows "$\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta$". Since $F$ is given, existence is not the issue;
only **membership in $L_\theta$** is. Three cases.

**$\xi = 0$.** $\mathrm{gr}(F,0) = \emptyset$, and $\emptyset \in L_\theta$ since $\theta$ is a
limit.

**$\xi = \zeta+1$.**
$\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta+1) = \mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta) \cup \{\langle\zeta,F(\zeta)\rangle\}$. The
induction hypothesis gives $\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta) \in L_\theta$, the step hypothesis gives
$F(\zeta) \in L_\theta$, and $\zeta < \theta$ gives $\zeta \in L_\theta$. Closure of $L_\theta$
under pairing and $\mathrm{insert}$ gives the conclusion. This corresponds to the paper's
"build $s \cup \{\langle\xi,y\rangle\}$".

**$\xi = \lambda$ a limit.** This is the longest case, in three steps.

*Step A: build the partial approximation at each stage.* For each $\xi_0 < \lambda$, the
induction hypothesis gives $\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi_0) \in L_\theta$ and the step hypothesis gives
$\Phi_\theta(\zeta,\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta),F(\zeta))$ for each $\zeta < \xi_0$. Letting
$b_{\xi_0} \in L_\theta$ be the finite set collecting those witnesses,
$\mathrm{PA}(\xi_0,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi_0),b_{\xi_0})$ holds.

*Step B: bundle with $\Sigma_1$-Collection.* The witness block of the predicate

$$
R(\xi_0)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists g\ \exists b\ \mathrm{PA}(\xi_0,g,b)
$$

is the two variables $(g,b)$, and $\mathrm{PA}$ is $\Delta_0$. Applying the
$\Sigma_1$-Collection of [P-05](P-05.md) over $\xi_0 \in \lambda$ yields $B \in L_\theta$ with

$$
\forall \xi_0 < \lambda\ \exists g \in B\ \exists b \in B\ \mathrm{PA}(\xi_0,g,b).
$$

This corresponds to the paper's "Corollary 10.2 collects a **function** $G$ with
$G(\xi) = s_\xi$", but Lean takes a **set** $B$ rather than a function. Since uniqueness (a) is
available, no functionality is needed to extract the required $g$ from $B$.

*Step C: separate out only the partial approximations with $\Delta_0$-Separation.*

$$
G := \{g \in B : \exists \xi_0 \in \lambda\ \exists b \in B\ \mathrm{PA}(\xi_0,g,b)\}
$$

is built by Separation. $\mathrm{PA}$ is $\Delta_0$, $\exists\xi_0$ is bounded by $\lambda$ and
$\exists b$ by $B$, so the Separation is legitimate. Since $\theta$ is a limit,
$G \in L_\theta$.

*Step D: the union equals the graph.* Show

$$
\mathrm{gr}(F,\lambda) = \bigcup G
$$

by double inclusion. $\subseteq$: let $p = \langle\xi',F(\xi')\rangle$ with $\xi' < \lambda$.
Since $\lambda$ is a limit, $\xi'+1 < \lambda$. Take $g \in B$ and $b \in B$ with
$\mathrm{PA}(\xi'+1,g,b)$ from Step B; uniqueness (a) gives $g = \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi'+1)$, so
$g \in G$ and $p \in g$. $\supseteq$: if $p \in g \in G$ then by the definition of $G$ there is
$\xi_0 < \lambda$ with $\mathrm{PA}(\xi_0,g,b)$, and (a) gives $g = \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi_0)$, so
$p = \langle\xi',F(\xi')\rangle$ with $\xi' < \xi_0 < \lambda$.

Since $\theta$ is a limit, $\bigcup G \in L_\theta$. This corresponds to the paper's
"$s = \bigcup \mathrm{ran}(G)$", but Lean's $G$ is a set of partial approximations rather than
a function, so no $\mathrm{ran}$ is needed and one $\bigcup$ suffices.

#### (c) Claim (2): unique existence

(1) gives $\mathrm{gr}(F,\beta) \in L_\theta$, and this is the required $G$. $\mathrm{Fun}$,
$\mathrm{Dom} = \beta$ and the recursion equation follow at once from the definition of the
graph and the step hypothesis. Uniqueness: if $G'$ satisfies the same conditions then $G'$ is a
partial approximation on $\beta$, so applying (a) with $\xi := \beta$ gives
$G' = \mathrm{gr}(F,\beta)$.

#### (d) Claim (3): building $F$

The paper concludes "there is exactly one function $F$", so $F$ is an object produced in the
conclusion. Lean's (1) and (2) received $F$ as a hypothesis, so a stage building $F$ is added.

By external well-founded recursion, define

$$
F(\xi) :=
\begin{cases}
\text{some } y \text{ with } \Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),y) & (\text{if one exists}) \\
\emptyset & (\text{otherwise}).
\end{cases}
$$

Since $<$ on the ordinals is well-founded, this recursion is legitimate. Take as lemmas the
unfolding equation

$$
F(\xi) = \text{if } \exists y\ \Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),y) \text{ then } (\text{that choice}) \text{ else } \emptyset
$$

and the fact that when one exists, $\Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),F(\xi))$ does hold (the
specification of the choice).

Next show, by well-founded induction on $\xi$,

$$
\forall \xi \le \beta\ \ \bigl(\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta \wedge (\xi < \beta \to \Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),F(\xi)))\bigr).
$$

Fix $\xi$; the induction hypothesis makes $\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta) \in L_\theta$ and
$\Phi_\theta(\zeta,\mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta),F(\zeta))$ available for each $\zeta < \xi$. Then the
paper's hypothesis (if $g$ is a function on $\zeta$ satisfying the recursion equation then
$\exists! y$) can be applied with $g := \mathrm{gr}(F,\zeta)$, giving unique existence at every
$\zeta < \xi$. Using this as the hypothesis of (1) gives $\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi) \in L_\theta$.
Moreover, if $\xi < \beta$, applying the hypothesis with $g := \mathrm{gr}(F,\xi)$ gives
$\exists y$, so the specification of the choice gives
$\Phi_\theta(\xi,\mathrm{gr}(F,\xi),F(\xi))$.

Finally, feed this $F$ into (2) to obtain the conclusion.

Where the paper shows "a partial approximation exists" by Set Induction and thereby builds $F$,
Lean builds $F$ first by **external well-founded recursion and choice** and then shows only
that its values land in $L_\theta$, using $\Delta_0$-Collection. It does not construct $F$
inside $\mathrm{KP}$.

#### (e) Claim (4): $\omega$-recursion

The paper writes "extend the rule to $\omega+1$, fix the last value, apply this result and then
restrict to $\omega$". Lean has made the upper bound $\beta$ a general ordinal from the start,
so it is enough to substitute $\beta := \omega$. That $\omega < \theta$ is the first condition
of admissibility, and $\mathrm{Dom}(G) = \omega$ holds because the image of $\beta$ is $\omega$
itself. Neither extension nor restriction is needed.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) How $F$ is built

The paper shows by Set Induction that "a partial approximation with domain $\lambda$ exists for
every $\lambda \le \eta+1$" and obtains $F$ by taking $\lambda = \eta+1$. Lean builds $F$ first
by external well-founded recursion and choice, and shows only that the graph of its values lands
in $L_\theta$, using $\Delta_0$-Collection. It does not construct $F$ inside $\mathrm{KP}$. This
is the same root as (i) of [P-04](P-04.md).

The limit case is handled differently as well. The paper collects by Corollary 10.2 a
**function** $G$ with $G(\xi) = s_\xi$ and takes $\bigcup\mathrm{ran}(G)$, whereas Lean takes a
**set** $B$ by $\Sigma_1$-Collection, separates out the set $G$ of partial approximations and
takes $\bigcup G$. This is because there is no counterpart to Corollary 10.2
([P-05](P-05.md)). Since partial approximations are unique, functionality is not needed.

The $\omega$-recursion is built differently too. The paper extends the rule to $\omega+1$, fixes
the last value and restricts to $\omega$. Lean has made the upper bound $\beta$ a general
ordinal from the start, so it is enough to substitute $\beta := \omega$. Here Lean is the
shorter one.

### (ii) The $\omega$-recursion is not used downstream

At the two places where the paper uses $\omega$-recursion — the construction of the sequences
$(Q_n)$ and $(R_n)$ in Lemma 10.4(1)(2) ([P-07](P-07.md)) and the construction of
$T = \bigcup_n T_n$ in Lemma 10.5(1) ([P-08](P-08.md)) — Lean uses external induction on
$\mathbb{N}$ together with $\Sigma_1$-Collection. The theorem in claim (4) above exists but is
not used there.

Switching over would take a revision of the same size as [P-08](P-08.md), 800 to 1,200 lines in
total.
