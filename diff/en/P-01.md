# P-01: (8.2) the well-formedness code $\mathrm{Form}(e)$

## The paper

### Statement

(8.2)

$$
\mathrm{Form}(e) \quad :\Longleftrightarrow \quad \exists s \in \omega\ (s \text{ is a well-formedness derivation of } e)
$$

The formula constructors are fixed in (8.1) as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Eq}(i,j) &= \pi(0,\pi(i,j)), &
\mathrm{In}(i,j) &= \pi(1,\pi(i,j)), &
\mathrm{Neg}(e) &= \pi(2,e), \\
\mathrm{Conj}(e,d) &= \pi(3,\pi(e,d)), &
\mathrm{Ex}(i,e) &= \pi(4,\pi(i,e)) &&
\end{aligned}
$$

where $\pi$ is the Cantor pairing function

$$
\pi(m,n) = \frac{(m+n)(m+n+1)}{2} + n
$$

and a finite sequence of naturals $(a_0,\dots,a_{r-1})$ is coded as the pair of the length $r$
and $\pi(a_0,\pi(a_1,\dots,\pi(a_{r-1},0)\dots))$.

### Proof

A well-formedness derivation is a finite sequence $s$ of naturals each of whose terms is an
atomic formula code of (8.1) or is obtained by applying one constructor to earlier terms of the
same sequence, and whose last term is the code in question. The condition "$s$ is a
well-formedness derivation of $e$" only inspects each position of the sequence and the tags and
projections of the constructors, and can be written as a $\Delta_0$ formula with all
quantification bounded by $s, e, \omega$. So we define

$$\mathrm{Form}(e) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists s \in \omega\ (s \text{ is a well-formedness derivation of } e).$$

Since a formula has a finite syntax tree, every ordinary well-formedness code satisfies (8.2);
conversely, by induction on the length of the derivation, a code satisfying (8.2) is a
well-formed formula built in finitely many steps from (8.1).

## Lean

Below, $\bar n$ denotes the von Neumann natural $\{0,1,\dots,n-1\}$ and $\langle x,y\rangle$
the Kuratowski pair $\{\{x\},\{x,y\}\}$. $L_\omega$ is the class of hereditarily finite sets.

### The statement in Lean

**The codes** use a different tagging from (8.1) and are given as hereditarily finite sets. The
primitives are the five $\bot,\ =,\ \in,\ \to,\ \forall$, and
$\neg,\wedge,\exists$ are derived.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\ulcorner \bot \urcorner &= \langle \bar 0, \bar 0\rangle, &
\ulcorner v_i = v_j \urcorner &= \langle \bar 1, \langle \bar i, \bar j\rangle\rangle, &
\ulcorner v_i \in v_j \urcorner &= \langle \bar 2, \langle \bar i, \bar j\rangle\rangle, \\
\ulcorner \varphi \to \psi \urcorner &= \langle \bar 3, \langle \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner, \ulcorner\psi\urcorner\rangle\rangle, &
\ulcorner \forall v_i\,\varphi \urcorner &= \langle \bar 4, \langle \bar i, \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner\rangle\rangle. &&
\end{aligned}
$$

**Atomic formula codes** ($w$ is the set the variable indices live in; $w = \omega$ is
substituted):

$$
\mathrm{Atom}_w(e) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
e = \langle \bar 0,\bar 0\rangle \ \vee\
\exists i \in w\ \exists j \in w\
\bigl(e = \langle \bar 1,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle \vee e = \langle \bar 2,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle\bigr).
$$

**Derivations** ($h$ is the set the codes and the derivations live in; $h = L_\omega$ is
substituted):

$$
\mathrm{DerSeq}_w(s) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Fun}(s)\ \wedge\ \exists d \in w\ \mathrm{Dom}(s)=d\ \wedge\
\forall k\,\forall e\ \bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \to \Theta(k,e)\bigr)
$$

where $\Theta(k,e)$ is the following three-way choice.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Theta(k,e) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Atom}_w(e) \\
\vee\ &\exists k_1 \in k\ \exists k_2 \in k\ \exists e_1\,\exists e_2\
 \bigl(\langle k_1,e_1\rangle \in s \wedge \langle k_2,e_2\rangle \in s
 \wedge e = \langle \bar 3,\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\rangle\bigr) \\
\vee\ &\exists k_1 \in k\ \exists e_1\ \exists i \in w\
 \bigl(\langle k_1,e_1\rangle \in s \wedge e = \langle \bar 4,\langle i,e_1\rangle\rangle\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $k$ is a von Neumann natural, $k_1 \in k$ expresses "$k_1 < k$", that is, "an earlier
term".

**The last-term condition** ($d$ is the domain of $s$ and $k$ its greatest element):

$$
\mathrm{Last}_w(s,e) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\exists d \in w\ \Bigl(\mathrm{Dom}(s) = d \ \wedge\
\exists k \in d\ \bigl((\forall k' \in d\ (k' \in k \vee k' = k)) \wedge \langle k,e\rangle \in s\bigr)\Bigr).
$$

**The well-formedness test**:

$$
\mathrm{Form}(e) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists s \in h\ \bigl(\mathrm{DerSeq}_w(s) \wedge \mathrm{Last}_w(s,e)\bigr).
$$

There are two claims to prove.

1. $\Delta_0$-ness. $\mathrm{Form}$ is a $\Delta_0$ predicate with $\{h,w\}$ as parameters.
   More precisely, when the variable indices $h,w,e$ are assigned, there is a $\Delta_0$ formula
   over the support $\{h,w,e\}$ whose satisfaction agrees with $\mathrm{Form}$ for every good
   domain and valuation.
2. Correctness. When $h = L_\omega$ and $w = \omega$,

$$
\mathrm{Form}(e) \iff \exists \varphi\ (e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner).
$$

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) $\Delta_0$-ness

The paper settles it with "only inspects each position of the sequence and the tags and
projections of the constructors". In Lean, $s$ is a set of Kuratowski pairs rather than a
natural number, so **extracting a component is a descent along $\in$ rather than a
projection**. The quantifiers are therefore bounded via two rewriting lemmas.

$$
\forall k\,\forall e\ \bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \to P(k,e)\bigr)
\iff
\forall p \in s\ \forall q \in p\ \forall k \in q\ \forall q' \in p\ \forall e \in q'\
\bigl(p = \langle k,e\rangle \to P(k,e)\bigr)
$$

$$
\exists e\ \bigl(\langle k,e\rangle \in s \wedge P(e)\bigr)
\iff
\exists p \in s\ \exists q \in p\ \exists e \in q\
\bigl(p = \langle k,e\rangle \wedge P(e)\bigr)
$$

Both follow from $\langle k,e\rangle = \{\{k\},\{k,e\}\}$. Left to right takes
$p = \langle k,e\rangle$, $q = \{k\}$, $q' = \{k,e\}$; right to left just substitutes
$p = \langle k,e\rangle$.

After this rewriting, the $\Delta_0$-ness of $\mathrm{DerSeq}_w$ is obtained by assembling the
following pieces with $\wedge,\vee,\to$ and bounded quantification.

- $\mathrm{Fun}(s)$ is $\Delta_0$
- $\mathrm{Dom}(s) = d$ is $\Delta_0$
- $z = \langle x,y\rangle$ is $\Delta_0$ (the conjunction of
  $\forall u \in z\ (u=\{x\} \vee u=\{x,y\})$ with
  $\{x\} \in z \wedge \{x,y\} \in z$)
- $e = \langle \bar n, \langle a,b\rangle\rangle$ is $\Delta_0$ (one for each tag $\bar n$)
- $x = \bar k$ is $\Delta_0$ ($k$ is an external natural, so the expansion of $\bar k$ is
  written out)

The $\Delta_0$-ness of $\mathrm{Last}_w$ is assembled in the following five steps, assigning
the variable $m$ to $d$, $m+1$ to $k$ and $m+2$ to $k'$.

$$
\underbrace{(k' \in k) \vee (k' = k)}_{\text{two atomic formulas}}
\ \xrightarrow{\ \forall k' \in d\ }\ \cdot
\ \xrightarrow{\ \wedge\ \langle k,e\rangle \in s\ }\ \cdot
\ \xrightarrow{\ \exists k \in d\ }\ \cdot
\ \xrightarrow{\ \wedge\ \mathrm{Dom}(s)=d\ }\ \cdot
\ \xrightarrow{\ \exists d \in w\ }\ \mathrm{Last}_w(s,e)
$$

The support shrinks as $\{m+2,m+1\} \to \{m,m+1\} \to \{m,m+1,s,e\} \to \{m,s,e\}
\to \{s,m,e\} \to \{w,s,e\}$, ending at $\{w,s,e\}$. For $\langle k,e\rangle \in s$, the
$\Delta_0$ predicate "$\langle x,y\rangle \in f$" is used directly rather than the second
rewriting lemma above.

Finally,

$$
\mathrm{Form}(e) = \exists s \in h\ \bigl(\mathrm{DerSeq}_w(s) \wedge \mathrm{Last}_w(s,e)\bigr)
$$

just puts $\exists s \in h$ on the conjunction of $\mathrm{DerSeq}$ and $\mathrm{Last}$, and
the support becomes $\{h,w,e\}$.

#### (b) Correctness, direction $\Leftarrow$

Build an explicit derivation from $\varphi$. First define the **list of subformula codes** of
$\varphi$ by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{der}(\bot) &= [\ulcorner\bot\urcorner], \\
\mathrm{der}(v_i = v_j) &= [\ulcorner v_i = v_j\urcorner], \\
\mathrm{der}(v_i \in v_j) &= [\ulcorner v_i \in v_j\urcorner], \\
\mathrm{der}(\varphi \to \psi) &= \mathrm{der}(\varphi) \frown \mathrm{der}(\psi) \frown [\ulcorner\varphi\to\psi\urcorner], \\
\mathrm{der}(\forall v_i\,\varphi) &= \mathrm{der}(\varphi) \frown [\ulcorner\forall v_i\,\varphi\urcorner]
\end{aligned}
$$

and define the map sending a list $l = [x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}]$ to a set by

$$
\mathrm{seq}_k([\,]) = \emptyset, \qquad
\mathrm{seq}_k(x \frown l) = \{\langle \bar k, x\rangle\} \cup \mathrm{seq}_{k+1}(l).
$$

Put $s := \mathrm{seq}_0(\mathrm{der}(\varphi))$. Three things are to be shown.

**(b-1) $s \in L_\omega$.** By induction on the length of the list. $L_\omega$ contains the
empty set and is closed under $\mathrm{insert}$ and Kuratowski pairing. Each term
$x \in \mathrm{der}(\varphi)$ is the code of some $\psi$ (by structural induction on
$\varphi$), and codes are elements of $L_\omega$ (each clause of $\ulcorner\cdot\urcorner$ is
built from $\bar n$ and pairs, and $\bar n \in L_\omega$).

**(b-2) $\mathrm{DerSeq}_\omega(s)$.** Show the four conditions in turn.

- $\mathrm{Fun}(s)$: if $\langle \bar a,b\rangle,\langle \bar a,b'\rangle \in s$ then, by the
  definition of $\mathrm{seq}$, $l[a] = b$ and $l[a] = b'$, so $b = b'$.
- $\mathrm{Dom}(s) = \overline{|\mathrm{der}(\varphi)|} \in \omega$: both directions from
  $a \in \bar n \iff \exists m < n\ (a = \bar m)$ and
  $\langle \bar m, x\rangle \in \mathrm{seq}_0(l) \iff l[m] = x$.
- the local condition: show by structural induction on $\varphi$ that the value at position $n$
  is either atomic or built from earlier positions. Write the property of a position as

$$
\mathrm{Good}(l,n) :\Longleftrightarrow \exists x\ \bigl(l[n] = x \wedge
(\mathrm{Atom}_\omega(x) \vee \Xi_{\to}(l,n,x) \vee \Xi_{\forall}(l,n,x))\bigr)
$$

$$
\Xi_{\to}(l,n,x) :\Longleftrightarrow \exists n_1 < n\ \exists n_2 < n\ \exists x_1\,x_2\
(l[n_1]=x_1 \wedge l[n_2]=x_2 \wedge x = \langle \bar 3,\langle x_1,x_2\rangle\rangle)
$$

$$
\Xi_{\forall}(l,n,x) :\Longleftrightarrow \exists n_1 < n\ \exists x_1\ \exists i\
(l[n_1]=x_1 \wedge x = \langle \bar 4,\langle \bar i,x_1\rangle\rangle).
$$

  $\mathrm{Good}$ has the following two stability properties under concatenation.

$$
\mathrm{Good}(l_1,n) \Rightarrow \mathrm{Good}(l_1 \frown l_2, n), \qquad
\mathrm{Good}(l_2,n) \Rightarrow \mathrm{Good}(l_1 \frown l_2, |l_1| + n).
$$

  The first holds because $n < |l_1|$ leaves the indices unchanged; the second shifts every
  index by $|l_1|$ and follows from $n_1 < n \Rightarrow |l_1|+n_1 < |l_1|+n$. Using these,
  show $\forall n\ \mathrm{Good}(\mathrm{der}(\varphi),n)$ by structural induction on
  $\varphi$.

  - $\varphi$ is $\bot,\ v_i=v_j,\ v_i \in v_j$: $\mathrm{der}$ has length 1, so only $n=0$
    occurs and its value is an atomic formula code.
  - $\varphi = \psi_1 \to \psi_2$: put $|\mathrm{der}(\psi_1)| = p$ and
    $|\mathrm{der}(\psi_2)| = q$.
    - $n < p$: apply left-concatenation stability once to
      $\mathrm{Good}(\mathrm{der}(\psi_1),n)$.
    - $p \le n < p+q$: apply left-concatenation of $[\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner]$ to
      $\mathrm{Good}(\mathrm{der}(\psi_2),n-p)$, then right-concatenation of
      $\mathrm{der}(\psi_1)$.
    - $n = p+q$: the value is $\ulcorner\psi_1 \to \psi_2\urcorner = \langle\bar 3,
      \langle\ulcorner\psi_1\urcorner,\ulcorner\psi_2\urcorner\rangle\rangle$, and one takes
      $n_1 := p-1 < n$ and $n_2 := p+q-1 < n$. Indeed
      $\mathrm{der}(\psi_1)[p-1] = \ulcorner\psi_1\urcorner$ and
      $\mathrm{der}(\psi_2)[q-1] = \ulcorner\psi_2\urcorner$ (the "last term" lemma of (b-3)
      below), and the indices are nonnegative since $p,q \ge 1$.
  - $\varphi = \forall v_i\,\psi$: $n < |\mathrm{der}(\psi)|$ is left-concatenation stability,
    and $n = |\mathrm{der}(\psi)|$ takes $n_1 := n-1$.

  Finally, translating the index condition $n_1 < n$ of $\mathrm{Good}$ into
  $\bar{n_1} \in \bar n$ and $l[m] = x$ into $\langle \bar m,x\rangle \in s$ gives the local
  condition.

**(b-3) $\mathrm{Last}_\omega(s,\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)$.** Take
$d := \overline{|\mathrm{der}(\varphi)|}$ and $k := \overline{|\mathrm{der}(\varphi)|-1}$.

- $d \in \omega$ and $\mathrm{Dom}(s) = d$ were shown in (b-2).
- $k \in d$ follows from $|\mathrm{der}(\varphi)| \ge 1$. The length is positive because every
  clause of $\mathrm{der}$ is nonempty.
- maximality $\forall k' \in d\ (k' \in k \vee k' = k)$: if $k' \in \bar n$ then $k' = \bar m$
  with $m < n$. If $m < n-1$ then $\bar m \in \overline{n-1}$; if $m = n-1$ then
  $\bar m = \overline{n-1}$.
- $\langle k,\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner\rangle \in s$: show
  $\mathrm{der}(\varphi)[\,|\mathrm{der}(\varphi)|-1\,] = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$ by cases on
  $\varphi$. For $\bot,=,\in$ the length is 1 and it is clear. For $\psi_1 \to \psi_2$ the
  trailing $[\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner]$ is the last position, and likewise for
  $\forall v_i\,\psi$.

#### (c) Correctness, direction $\Rightarrow$

Unfolding $\mathrm{Form}(e)$ gives $s \in L_\omega$, $\mathrm{DerSeq}_\omega(s)$,
$d \in \omega$, $\mathrm{Dom}(s) = d$, $k \in d$ and $\langle k,e\rangle \in s$. The maximality
conjunct is not used. From $d \in \omega$ we get $d = \bar N$, and from $k \in \bar N$ we get
$k = \bar n$ with $n < N$. So what has to be shown is

$$
\forall n\ \forall e\ \bigl(\langle \bar n, e\rangle \in s \to \exists \varphi\ (e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner)\bigr),
$$

and this is shown by **strong induction** on $n$. Applying the local condition of
$\mathrm{DerSeq}$ to $\langle \bar n,e\rangle \in s$ splits into three cases.

- $\mathrm{Atom}_\omega(e)$: if $e = \langle\bar 0,\bar 0\rangle$ then $\varphi = \bot$. If
  $e = \langle\bar 1,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle$ with $i,j \in \omega$, then $i = \bar{i'}$ and
  $j = \bar{j'}$ and $\varphi = (v_{i'} = v_{j'})$. The case $\langle\bar 2,\cdot\rangle$ gives
  $\varphi = (v_{i'} \in v_{j'})$ likewise.
- $e = \langle\bar 3,\langle e_1,e_2\rangle\rangle$ with $k_1,k_2 \in \bar n$ and
  $\langle k_1,e_1\rangle,\langle k_2,e_2\rangle \in s$: $k_1 = \overline{n_1}$ with $n_1 < n$,
  and likewise $n_2 < n$. The induction hypothesis gives $e_1 = \ulcorner\varphi_1\urcorner$
  and $e_2 = \ulcorner\varphi_2\urcorner$, so $\varphi = \varphi_1 \to \varphi_2$.
- $e = \langle\bar 4,\langle i,e_1\rangle\rangle$ with $k_1 \in \bar n$ and $i \in \omega$:
  $n_1 < n$ and the induction hypothesis give $e_1 = \ulcorner\varphi_1\urcorner$ and
  $i = \bar{i'}$, so $\varphi = \forall v_{i'}\,\varphi_1$.

The paper's "induction on the length of the derivation" becomes **strong induction on the
position $n$** in Lean. It runs on the position rather than the length because the local
condition of $\mathrm{DerSeq}$ expresses "an earlier position" by $k_1 \in k$, which can be fed
to the induction hypothesis directly.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There is one difference: the bounding range is $L_\omega$ rather than $\omega$.

A derivation $s$ is a list of formulas. In the paper a formula is a natural number, so the list
too collapses into a single natural number by Cantor pairing and can be written $s \in \omega$.
In Lean a formula is a hereditarily finite set built from Kuratowski pairs, so the list is a
finite function "index $\mapsto$ formula", that is, a set. A set is not an element of $\omega$,
so the class $L_\omega$ of all hereditarily finite sets is used as the place where $s$ lives.
$L_\omega$ plays the same role as the paper's $\omega$; its range is not wider.

This range cannot be dropped.

- The object language is the pure $\{\in\}$ language with no constant symbols. The $\Delta_0$
  framework has the shape "give values to the variables of the support by a valuation", so
  there is structurally no place to put $L_\omega$ as a constant.
- $L_\omega$ is not $\Delta_0$-definable. There is no way but to pass it as a parameter.
- Dropping it would make $\exists s$ an unbounded quantifier and break the $\Delta_0$-ness of
  (8.2).

The range being $L_\omega$ propagates as far as satisfaction codes, assignments and the
free-variable test, and the fixed parameters of the $\Delta_0$ formulas become the two
$(\omega, L_\omega)$ instead of the paper's single $\omega$. The same difference appears in
[P-03](P-03.md), [P-09](P-09.md) and [P-13](P-13.md).

To make the medium $\mathbb{N}$, one would need the $\Delta_0$-ness of the graph of the Cantor
pairing $\pi$ on $\omega$, the two projections, the length, components and concatenation of
finite sequences, and an internalization of primitive recursion by finite computation
sequences. That is 3,000 to 5,000 new lines, plus rewriting the 23 files and 14,473 lines that
touch the codes.
