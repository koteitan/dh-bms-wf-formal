# P-10: Lemma 12.2, safe syntactic operations

## The paper

### Statement

Lemma 12.2 (safe syntactic operations). Under the fixed coding, the graphs of the following
operations are $\Delta_0$.

1. Variable renaming, substitution, avoidance of bound variable capture, relativization,
   prefixing an alternating block.
2. Dualization of $\hat\Sigma_q$ and $\hat\Pi_q$.
3. Padding, moving a $\hat\Sigma_j$ formula with $j \le q$ to an equivalent $\hat\Sigma_q$
   formula, and a $\hat\Pi_j$ formula to an equivalent $\hat\Pi_q$ formula.
4. Collecting a finite conjunction or disjunction of formulas of the same alternation type into
   a single formula of the same alternation type.

The equivalences hold in every nonempty first-order structure.

### Proof

All of them are primitive recursions on the finite syntax tree. In padding, one puts as many
vacuous blocks as needed at the innermost position, quantifying variables that do not occur in
the formula. In a nonempty structure, if the variable $z$ does not occur in $\phi$ then

$$\exists z\,\phi \leftrightarrow \phi, \qquad \forall z\,\phi \leftrightarrow \phi$$

so the meaning is unchanged.

We explain the finite joining. Rename the bound variables of the two formulas apart. For

$$Q_1 \vec x_1 \cdots Q_q \vec x_q\,\delta, \qquad Q_1 \vec u_1 \cdots Q_q \vec u_q\,\varepsilon$$

with the same polarity list $Q_1,\dots,Q_q$, take, for $\star \in \{\wedge,\vee\}$,

$$Q_1(\vec x_1,\vec u_1) \cdots Q_q(\vec x_q,\vec u_q)(\delta \star \varepsilon). \tag{12.1}$$

That (12.1) is equivalent to the $\star$-join of the two original formulas follows by induction
processing the quantifiers one at a time from the outermost block. It merely merges two
existential quantifications, or two universal ones, of the same polarity over mutually
independent variables into one block. When showing the converse direction for the combination
of disjunction and universal quantification, if one side is not universally true, fix one
counterexample value and one obtains that the other side is true for every value. The case of
existential quantification and conjunction is the same. Nonemptiness is used only to choose a
value for an unneeded variable. The finite case iterates the binary operation.

We also quote Remark 12.3.

The syntactic operations of Lemma 12.2 do not include, for instance, moving the bounded
quantifier $\forall u \in a$ of $\forall u \in a\ \exists x\ \delta(u,x)$ outside an unbounded
existential quantifier. Transforming this formula into a single simple $\Sigma_1$ prenex form
can require Collection, so the truth predicates and reflection sentences of this paper are
constructed in a form not requiring that move.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

The eight operations are written item by item, all using the notation of [P-02](P-02.md). Here
$\bar n$ is the von Neumann natural and $\ulcorner\cdot\urcorner$ the code.

#### 12.2(1)-a Variable renaming

Let $\nu$ be "the code of the finite function giving the correspondence of variable indices".
The local rules rewrite the indices according to $\nu$ at an atomic formula, pair the
children's values at $\to$, and rewrite $i$ as well at $\forall v_i$.

$$
\mathrm{Ren}(\nu,e,d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ G_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{ren}},\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{ren}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{ren}}}(\nu,e,d)
$$

**$\Delta_0$-ness**: the three local rules are $\Delta_0$, so it follows from the
$\Delta_0$-ness of $G$ in [P-02](P-02.md).

**Correctness**: if $\nu$ represents a map $f$ and covers every variable occurring in
$\varphi$, then

$$
\mathrm{Ren}(\nu,\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,d) \iff d = \ulcorner \varphi[f] \urcorner.
$$

#### 12.2(1)-b Substitution

The semantic side is defined first. Writing $\mathrm{bv}(\varphi)$ for the set of bound
variables and $\mathrm{sb}(x,y,i) := (i = x\ ?\ y : i)$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bot[x{:=}y] &= \bot, \\
(v_i = v_j)[x{:=}y] &= v_{\mathrm{sb}(x,y,i)} = v_{\mathrm{sb}(x,y,j)}, \\
(v_i \in v_j)[x{:=}y] &= v_{\mathrm{sb}(x,y,i)} \in v_{\mathrm{sb}(x,y,j)}, \\
(\varphi\to\psi)[x{:=}y] &= \varphi[x{:=}y] \to \psi[x{:=}y], \\
(\forall v_i\,\varphi)[x{:=}y] &= \begin{cases}
\forall v_x\,\varphi & (i = x) \\
\forall v_i\,(\varphi[x{:=}y]) & (i \ne x)
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

Blocking the substitution when $i = x$ is the treatment of binding.

On the code side, with the parameter $p = \langle \bar x,\bar y\rangle$,

$$
\mathrm{Subst}(p,e,d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
G_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{sb}},\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{sb}},\mathcal{U}_{\mathrm{sb}}}(p,e,d')
$$

(for $d'$ see below). **$\Delta_0$-ness** and **correctness**:

$$
\mathrm{Subst}(\langle\bar x,\bar y\rangle,e,d) \iff
\exists\varphi\ \bigl(e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner \wedge d = \ulcorner\varphi[x{:=}y]\urcorner\bigr).
$$

The semantic equivalence is

$$
y \notin \mathrm{bv}(\varphi) \ \Longrightarrow\
\bigl(D \models \varphi[x{:=}y][v] \iff D \models \varphi[v[x \mapsto v(y)]]\bigr)
$$

and it requires **no condition on the domain $D$ whatsoever** (not even nonemptiness).

#### 12.2(1)-c Avoidance of bound variable capture

**No counterpart.**

#### 12.2(1)-d Relativization

The semantic side is shared with [P-13](P-13.md): only the unbounded quantifiers are restricted
to $M$. The syntactic test for being a bounded quantifier is

$$
\mathrm{isBall}(i,\varphi) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists j\,\exists\psi\ (j \ne i \wedge \varphi = (v_i \in v_j \to \psi))
$$

and

$$
(\forall v_i\,\varphi)^{M} =
\begin{cases}
\forall v_i\,(\varphi^{M}) & (\mathrm{isBall}(i,\varphi)) \\
\forall v_i \in M\ (\varphi^{M}) & (\text{otherwise}).
\end{cases}
$$

On the code side, build the $\Delta_0$ predicate carrying out the $\mathrm{isBall}$ test on
codes,

$$
\mathrm{isBall}^\sharp(i,e)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\exists j \in w\ \exists q \in e\ \exists t \in q\ \exists s \in t\ \exists c \in s\
\bigl(j \ne i \wedge e = \langle\bar 3,\langle\langle\bar 2,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle,c\rangle\rangle\bigr),
$$

and use it in the local rule for $\forall$. Bounding $\exists c$ by $e$ takes a four-level
$\in$ descent.

$$
\mathrm{RelTo}(\bar m,e,d) \iff \exists\varphi\ \bigl(e = \ulcorner\varphi\urcorner \wedge d = \ulcorner\varphi^{v_m}\urcorner\bigr).
$$

#### 12.2(1)-e Prefixing an alternating block

The code of a block formula is $\langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle$ (an $\exists$ block)
or $\langle\bar 2,\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle$ (a $\forall$ block), so prefixing is a single
tagged-pair equation.

$$
\mathrm{PrefixBlk}_t(\nu,e,d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ d = \langle\bar t,\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle
\qquad (t \in \{1,2\})
$$

This is $\Delta_0$. Correctness is $d = \ulcorner\exists\vec l\,\psi\urcorner$ ($t=1$) and
$d = \ulcorner\forall\vec l\,\psi\urcorner$ ($t=2$).

#### 12.2(2) Dualization

Defined by external recursion on $q$. The base (the $\Delta_0$ matrix) flips the sign bit $sg$
of the code $\langle\bar 0,\langle sg,d\rangle\rangle$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{DualSig}_0(e,d) &:\Longleftrightarrow \mathrm{DualDelta}(e,d), \\
\mathrm{DualSig}_{q+1}(e,d) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\exists \nu\,\exists e'\,\exists d'\ \bigl(e = \langle\bar 1,\langle\nu,e'\rangle\rangle \wedge
d = \langle\bar 2,\langle\nu,d'\rangle\rangle \wedge \mathrm{DualPi}_q(e',d')\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

($\mathrm{DualPi}$ swaps $\bar 1$ and $\bar 2$).

**$\Delta_0$-ness**: by induction on $q$. The $\Delta_0$-ness is a different formula for each
$q$.

**Correctness**:

$$
\mathrm{DualSig}_q(e,d) \iff
\exists b\ \bigl(\hat\Sigma_q(b) \wedge b \text{ has distinct variables inside blocks} \wedge
e = \ulcorner b\urcorner \wedge d = \ulcorner b^\ast\urcorner\bigr)
$$

The semantic equivalence $D \models b^\ast \iff D \not\models b$ holds with **no condition on
the domain**.

#### 12.2(3) Padding

Build in $\Delta_0$ form the predicate choosing a fresh variable,

$$
\mathrm{Fresh}(z,e)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
z \in w \wedge (\text{every index} \ge z \text{ is not free in } e) \wedge (\text{the index just below } z \text{ is free})
$$

($z$ is "the least index above the free variables of $e$"). Define by external recursion on $n$
the operation putting $n$ vacuous blocks at the innermost position, and

$$
\mathrm{PadSig}_{q,n}(e,d) \iff
\exists b\ \bigl(\hat\Sigma_q(b) \wedge \cdots \wedge e = \ulcorner b\urcorner \wedge d = \ulcorner \mathrm{pad}_n(b)\urcorner\bigr).
$$

The semantic equivalence requires **only that the domain be nonempty**. That is exactly the
paper's "$\exists z\,\phi \leftrightarrow \phi$".

#### 12.2(4) Joining finite conjunctions and disjunctions

**No counterpart.**

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) The common skeleton

Items a, b and d of 12.2(1) are built as instances of the schema of [P-02](P-02.md), in three
steps.

1. Write the local rules $\mathcal{A},\mathcal{I},\mathcal{U}$ and show each is $\Delta_0$.
2. Apply the $\Delta_0$-ness theorem of [P-02](P-02.md) to get, in one line, that the graph is
   $\Delta_0$.
3. Show correctness,
   $\mathrm{Graph}(\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner,d) \iff d = \ulcorner F(\varphi)\urcorner$, by
   structural induction on $\varphi$.

Step 3 is the body of the work and has two directions. $\Leftarrow$ explicitly constructs the
derivation and the computation sequence from $\varphi$. $\Rightarrow$ shows, by strong
induction on the position in the derivation, that "if the input at position $k$ is
$\ulcorner\psi\urcorner$ then the value at that position is $\ulcorner F(\psi)\urcorner$".

#### (b) Designing the trace values for substitution

Using the schema as it stands gets stuck in one place. The local rule for $\forall$,
$\mathcal{U}(w,p,i,v_1,v)$, can see only the bound variable $i$ and the **child's value**
$v_1$. But the $\forall$ clause of substitution,

$$
(\forall v_i\,\varphi)[x{:=}y] =
\begin{cases}
\forall v_x\,\varphi & (i = x) \\
\forall v_i\,(\varphi[x{:=}y]) & (i \ne x),
\end{cases}
$$

needs **the child's code itself** ($\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner$, before substitution) in the
case $i = x$. The child's value is $\ulcorner\varphi[x{:=}y]\urcorner$, which is not enough.

So the trace values are made **pairs**. The value at position $k$ is taken to be

$$
v_k := \langle \text{the input code at that position},\ \text{the output code at that position}\rangle.
$$

Then $\mathcal{U}$ can read the child's input code off the first component of $v_1$. The
definitions of $\mathrm{Trace}$ and $G$ are unchanged; only the shape of the values is changed.
The final graph is defined by peeling the pair on the output side,

$$
\mathrm{Subst}(p,e,d)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ G(p,e,\langle e,d\rangle).
$$

Relativization uses the same design, since its $\forall$ clause has to test on codes whether
the child has the form $v_i \in v_j \to \psi$.

Renaming does not need the child's input code, so its values can be the output codes
themselves.

#### (c) The syntactic test for relativization

The point is the $\Delta_0$-ness of $\mathrm{isBall}^\sharp(i,e)$. To see whether $e$ has the
form $\langle\bar 3,\langle\langle\bar 2,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle,c\rangle\rangle$, one has to
extract $j$ and $c$. $j$ can be bounded by $w$, but $c$ is taken from inside $e$ by an $\in$
descent. Since

$$
e = \{\{\bar 3\},\{\bar 3, \langle A,c\rangle\}\} \quad\text{where } A = \langle\bar 2,\langle i,j\rangle\rangle,
$$

one descends four levels: $\{\bar 3,\langle A,c\rangle\} \in e$,
$\langle A,c\rangle \in \{\bar 3,\langle A,c\rangle\}$,
$\{A,c\} \in \langle A,c\rangle$, $c \in \{A,c\}$. Hence it can be written in the form
$\exists q \in e\ \exists t \in q\ \exists s \in t\ \exists c \in s$.

Correctness,
$\mathrm{isBall}^\sharp(\bar i,\ulcorner\varphi\urcorner) \iff \mathrm{isBall}(i,\varphi)$,
follows from injectivity of the coding.

#### (d) $\Delta_0$-ness of dualization and padding

These two are not recursions on the syntax tree but are defined by **external recursion on the
number of blocks $q$ (or the number $n$ of added blocks)**. So their $\Delta_0$-ness is shown
by induction on $q$ as well.

The $\exists\nu,\exists e',\exists d'$ of

$$
\mathrm{DualSig}_{q+1}(e,d) \iff
\exists\nu\,\exists e'\,\exists d'\ (\text{two tagged-pair equations} \wedge \mathrm{DualPi}_q(e',d'))
$$

are bounded. $\nu$ and $e'$ are taken from $e$ and $d'$ from $d$ by $\in$ descent, so one
$\Delta_0$ lemma decomposing two codes at once is prepared and used. The induction hypothesis
makes $\mathrm{DualPi}_q$ $\Delta_0$, so the whole is $\Delta_0$.

Since a different formula comes out for each $q$, "$\mathrm{DualSig}$ is $\Delta_0$" is a
meta-level claim. The paper is the same, giving one operation for each $q$.

The $\mathrm{Fresh}$ of padding shows that "the least index above the free variables of $e$"
can be chosen uniquely with bounded quantification alone. The two conditions, that everything
$\ge z$ is non-free and that the index just below $z$ is free, can both be written with
$\forall k \in w$ and $\mathrm{NotFree}$, so they are $\Delta_0$.

#### (e) The range of validity of the semantic equivalences

The paper says "the equivalences hold in every nonempty first-order structure". The
corresponding lemmas in Lean take the domain $D$ as an argument, with the following conditions.

| Operation | Condition on the domain |
|---|---|
| substitution | **none** (not even nonemptiness) |
| dualization | **none** |
| padding | nonemptiness ($\exists x\ D(x)$) only |
| renaming | injectivity only (no condition on the domain) |

The paper requires nonemptiness only for the vacuous blocks of padding, so this agrees with the
paper.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) Two of the eight operations are not formalized

Avoidance of bound variable capture and the joining of finite conjunctions and disjunctions are
absent. Both are held up by one and the same gap. The substance of capture avoidance is the
renaming map

$$
\mathrm{avoid}(\varphi,K,i) = \begin{cases} i & (i \in \mathrm{fv}(\varphi)) \\ i+K & (\text{otherwise}),\end{cases}
$$

and its graph on codes needs $i' = i+K$, that is, a $\Delta_0$ graph for addition on $\omega$.
The tree has no $\Delta_0$ predicate for addition or order on the naturals at all (only the
successor and the order given by $\in$). The (12.1) of finite conjunction and disjunction uses
this same renaming to make the bound variables of two formulas disjoint, so it stops at the
same place.

Making addition $\Delta_0$ by a recursion on $\omega$ in the style of the schema of
[P-02](P-02.md) would be 250 to 350 new lines, and combining it with $\mathrm{NotFree}$ to
build the renaming map $\nu$ and feed it to $\mathrm{Ren}$ about 150 more.

Addition is not the only route. The $\mathrm{Fresh}$ of padding shows that a fresh variable can
be chosen uniquely with bounded quantification alone, so replacing $\mathrm{avoid}$ by a scheme
that takes fresh variables one at a time might make it writable without addition. In that case
the semantic side of capture avoidance would have to be rebuilt too.

### (ii) Generalization to arbitrary first-order structures

The paper says "the equivalences hold in every nonempty first-order structure". Lean's
satisfaction relation lives on a nonempty subclass $(D, \in\!\restriction\!D)$ of $V$, so
non-well-founded and non-extensional structures are out of range. The paper never uses such a
structure, so there is no actual harm, but it is not literally as printed.

Filling the gap would require defining satisfaction anew on a structure $(M,E)$ with an
arbitrary binary relation $E$ and mechanically reproving about 700 lines of lemmas other than
$\Delta_0$ absoluteness (changing valuations, block quantification, renaming, shifting bound
variables, $\exists$ and $\neg$, padding, joining conjunctions and disjunctions, and so on).
Transport is not enough: since $E$ is arbitrary, there is no general map embedding $(M,E)$ into
a subclass of $(V,\in)$. Generalizing the existing satisfaction relation itself would force a
rebuild of every SetTheory file, including the $L$ hierarchy and the $L$-codes.
