# P-13: Definition 14.2, the Tarski–Vaught formula

## The paper

### Statement

Definition 14.1 (alternating block elementarity). For transitive sets $M \subseteq N$ and
$q \ge 1$, $M \prec^*_q N$ means that $M$ and $N$ give the same truth value for every
$1 \le j \le q$, every $\hat\Sigma_j$ and $\hat\Pi_j$ formula and every $M$-parameter.

The condition gets stronger as $q$ grows, and $\prec^*_q$ is transitive. We write
$\mathrm{Tr}^M_{\hat\Sigma_j}(e,a)$ for the relativization of the fixed formula
$\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}$ obtained by restricting all of its unbounded quantifiers to $M$.
This is a $\Delta_0$ formula with $M$ as a parameter.

Definition 14.2 (the Tarski–Vaught formula). For $q \ge 1$, let $\mathrm{TV}_q(M)$ be one
standard $\hat\Pi_q$ presentation, given by Lemma 12.2, of the following finite conjunction:

$$
\bigwedge_{1 \le j \le q}
\forall e \in \omega\ \forall a \in M\
\bigl(\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_j}(e) \wedge \mathrm{Asn}_M(e,a) \wedge \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}(e,a)
\longrightarrow \mathrm{Tr}^M_{\hat\Sigma_j}(e,a)\bigr). \tag{14.1}
$$

Lemma 14.3 (complexity). $\mathrm{TV}_q \in \hat\Pi_q$.

### Proof

Proof of Lemma 14.3.

For a fixed $j$, the implication is a disjunction of the $\hat\Pi_j$ formula
$\neg \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}$ with a $\Delta_0$ formula, and can be collected into
$\hat\Pi_j$. The outer quantifications over $e \in \omega$ and $a \in M$ are bounded universal
quantifications. Writing them in pure block form as $\forall e(e \in \omega \to \cdots)$ and
$\forall a(a \in M \to \cdots)$ merely merges them into the existing leading universal block
and so does not increase the number of alternations. Padding raises each term to $\hat\Pi_q$,
and the finite conjunction is collected into one.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

Let $h$ be the set the codes live in ($h = L_\omega$), let $w$ be the set the variable indices
live in ($w = \omega$), and let $D$ be the domain.

**Each term of (14.1)**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{TVBody}_j(M)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Trans}(M) \to (\exists x\ x \in M) \to h \in M \to w \in M \to \\
&\forall e \in h\ \forall a \in M\
\bigl(\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_j}(e) \wedge \mathrm{Asn}^{BF}_M(e,a) \wedge
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}^{D}(e,a) \to \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}^{(\cdot \in M)}(e,a)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

**The finite conjunction**:

$$
\mathrm{TVConj}_0(M) := \top, \qquad
\mathrm{TVConj}_{j+1}(M) := \mathrm{TVConj}_j(M) \wedge \mathrm{TVBody}_{j+1}(M),
$$

$$
\mathrm{TV}_q(M) := \mathrm{TVConj}_q(M).
$$

**Relativization** is, as in the paper, "restricting only the unbounded quantifiers of the
fixed formula to $M$", but in Lean $\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}$ is a predicate taking the
domain $D$ as an argument, so substituting $D := (\cdot \in M)$ is used as the relativization.

**$\mathrm{Asn}^{BF}_M(e,a)$** is the paper's $\mathrm{Asn}_M(e,a)$, in the version for the
case where $e$ is the code of a block formula. It says that $a$ is a standard finite sequence
with values in $M$ covering all the free variables of $e$. The free variables of a block
formula are those of the $\Delta_0$ matrix minus the block variables, so it is defined by
recursion on the level $j$ of $\hat\Sigma_j$.

There is one claim to prove (Lemma 14.3).

$$
\mathrm{TV}_q \text{ is a } \hat\Pi_q \text{ predicate over the support } \{h,w,M\}.
$$

### The proof in Lean

Matching the four steps of the paper (collect the implication into $\hat\Pi_j$, merge the
outer bounded quantifiers into the leading block, raise to $\hat\Pi_q$ by padding, collect the
finite conjunction into one), Lean too builds it by induction on $q$. For the term with
$j = q+1$ it stacks from the inside out.

**Step 1: bring the relativization in the conclusion down to $\Delta_0$.**
$\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}$ is $\hat\Sigma_{q+1}$ (the complexity in [P-12](P-12.md)).
From the relativization theorem we extract the fact that its relativization
$\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}^{(\cdot\in M)}$ can be expressed by a $\Delta_0$ predicate with
$M$ as a parameter. That is, we obtain a $\Delta_0$ predicate $Q$ together with the
conditional equivalence

$$
\bigl(\text{$M$ transitive and nonempty, } h,w,e,a \in M\bigr) \to
\bigl(Q(v) \iff \mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}^{(\cdot\in M)}(e,a)\bigr).
$$

This condition is the source of the "hypotheses not in the paper" discussed below.

**Step 2: collect the implication into $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$.** $Q$ is $\Delta_0$, hence
$\hat\Pi_{q+1}$. An implication from a $\hat\Sigma_{q+1}$ predicate to a $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$
predicate is $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$ (by $\neg\hat\Sigma_{q+1} = \hat\Pi_{q+1}$ and closure under
disjunction). Hence

$$
\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}^{D}(e,a) \to Q(v)
$$

is $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$.

**Step 3: add the $\Delta_0$ antecedents.** $\mathrm{Asn}^{BF}_M(e,a)$ and
$\mathrm{Form}_{\hat\Sigma_{q+1}}(e)$ are both $\Delta_0$, so they are raised to
$\hat\Sigma_{q+1}$ and the implication is taken. This too is $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$.

**Step 4: add the outer quantifiers.** $e \in h$, $a \in M$, and the conditions for applying
the relativization ($M$ transitive and nonempty, $h,w \in M$) are all collected into one
$\Delta_0$ antecedent and added by implication to the $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$ predicate. This is the
part where the paper says "the outer quantifications over $e \in \omega$ and $a \in M$ are
bounded universal quantifications and merely merge into the leading universal block". In Lean
the bounded universal quantifiers $\forall e \in h$ and $\forall a \in M$ are handled by the
closure lemma for bounded universal quantification over $\hat\Pi$ predicates.

**Step 5: padding and the finite conjunction.** Each term with $j \le q+1$ is $\hat\Pi_j$, so
padding (12.2(3) of [P-10](P-10.md)) raises it to $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$. The induction hypothesis on
$q$ gives that $\mathrm{TVConj}_q$ is $\hat\Pi_q$, and this too is raised to $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$.
The conjunction of two $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$ predicates is $\hat\Pi_{q+1}$ (this is what 12.2(4)
amounts to, though in Lean a predicate-level conjunction lemma suffices).

The support becomes $\{h,w,M\}$.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are two differences.

### (i) The definition carries hypotheses the paper does not have

At the head of $\mathrm{TVBody}_j(M)$ there are four hypotheses attached as implications:

$$
\mathrm{Trans}(M) \to (\exists x\ x \in M) \to h \in M \to w \in M \to \cdots
$$

The paper's (14.1) has none of these.

They come from Step 1 above. Identifying the relativization $\mathrm{Tr}^M_{\hat\Sigma_j}$
with a $\Delta_0$ predicate $Q$ requires transitivity and nonemptiness of $M$ and membership of
the parameters in $M$, and these are carried into the definition.

This is the same defect as $\mathrm{Rel}_k$ in [P-14](P-14.md), and it can be fixed by the same
procedure.

1. Name the fixed formula of $\mathrm{Tr}_{\hat\Sigma_j}$ (extract by choice a formula
   representing the $\hat\Sigma_j$ predicate and exclude the variables used in the
   relativization from the bound variables)
2. Write $\mathrm{TVBody}_j(M)$ as the satisfaction of the relativized formula itself
3. Move the four hypotheses into the assumptions of the bridging lemma and discharge them from
   $M = L_\xi$ on the proof side of Theorem 14.4

Judging from what [P-14](P-14.md) took, this should be about 100 lines in a single file.

### (ii) There are two fixed parameters

The parameters of the paper's $\mathrm{TV}_q(M)$ are $M$ and the $\omega$ whose suppression is
declared in §8, two in all. Lean has $M$ and $(h,w) = (L_\omega,\omega)$, three in all, and the
support in Lemma 14.3 becomes $\{h,w,M\}$. The reason is the same as in [P-01](P-01.md).

The conclusion of Lemma 14.3 itself, "$\mathrm{TV}_q \in \hat\Pi_q$", does hold. That is the
only property used in the complexity of $\mathrm{St}_k$ in §15 and in the finite pattern
reflection of §17, so there is no effect downstream.
