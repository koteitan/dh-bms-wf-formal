# P-03: Definition 9.1, the $L$-hierarchy code

## The paper

### Statement

Definition 9.1 (the $L$-hierarchy code). $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$ says that, with

$$
\exists U \in \bigcup\bigcup c\ \ \exists H \in \bigcup\bigcup c\ \
\exists S \in \bigcup\bigcup c\ \ \exists D \in \bigcup\bigcup c,
$$

$\mathrm{FourCode}(c,U,H,S,D)$ and all of the following conditions hold.

1. $\eta$ is an ordinal, $U$ is transitive with $\eta, M, H, S, D, \omega \in U$, and
   $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$.
2. $H$ is a function on exactly $\eta+1$, $S$ and $D$ are functions on exactly $\eta$, and
   $H(0) = \emptyset$.
3. For each $\xi < \eta$, $\mathrm{SatCode}(H(\xi), U, S(\xi))$.
4. For each $\xi < \eta$, $D(\xi)$ is a function whose domain is exactly

$$
\{\langle e,a\rangle : e \in \omega,\ a \in U,\ \mathrm{DefInp}_{H(\xi)}(e,a)\}.
$$

   Its value $b = D(\xi)(e,a)$ is a subset of $H(\xi)$, and for every $x \in H(\xi)$,

$$
x \in b \iff \langle e, a_x\rangle \in S(\xi). \tag{9.1}
$$

5. For each $\xi < \eta$,

$$
H(\xi+1) = \mathrm{ran}\, D(\xi). \tag{9.2}
$$

   For a nonzero limit $\lambda \le \eta$,

$$
H(\lambda) = \bigcup_{\xi<\lambda} H(\xi). \tag{9.3}
$$

   Finally $M = H(\eta)$.

### Proof

It is a definition, so there is no proof. The paper's argument for $\Delta_0$-ness is as
follows.

The four leading existential quantifiers are bounded by $\bigcup\bigcup c$, and
$\mathrm{FourCode}$ is $\Delta_0$ as well. Moreover, from $H, S, D \in U$ and transitivity,
their function values $H(\xi), S(\xi), D(\xi)$ belong to $U$. By Lemma 8.2, every finite
parameter sequence made of elements of $H(\xi)$ and every concatenated assignment $a_x$ belong
to $U$ too. Being a function, having exactly a given domain, the values, the range and the
successor and limit conditions can all be unfolded by quantification bounded by
$\eta, U, H, S, D$. Hence

$$\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c) \in \Delta_0. \tag{9.4}$$

We also quote the following Lemma 9.2 (soundness of the code) and its proof, since they fix the
content of this definition.

Lemma 9.2 (soundness of the code). If $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$ then $M = L_\eta$.

Proof. Show

$$H(\xi) = L_\xi \tag{9.5}$$

by transfinite induction on $\xi \le \eta$. For $\xi = 0$ it is clear from the definition. At a
successor stage, Lemma 8.4 makes $S(\xi)$ the correct satisfaction relation of
$(H(\xi),\in)$. By condition (9.1), $\mathrm{ran}\,D(\xi)$ coincides exactly with the set of
all subsets of $H(\xi)$ that are first-order definable with finitely many parameters. Hence
$H(\xi+1) = \mathrm{Def}(H(\xi))$. Substituting the induction hypothesis gives
$H(\xi+1) = \mathrm{Def}(L_\xi) = L_{\xi+1}$. At a limit stage, (9.3) and the induction
hypothesis give $H(\lambda) = \bigcup L_\xi = L_\lambda$.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

Let $h$ be the set the codes live in (with $h = L_\omega$ substituted) and $w$ the set the
variable indices live in ($w = \omega$). Here $\bar n$ is the von Neumann natural,
$\langle x,y\rangle$ the Kuratowski pair and $\eta^+ := \eta \cup \{\eta\}$.

**The four-component code**:

$$
\mathrm{FourCode}(c,U,H,S,D)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Fun}(c) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(c) = \bar 4 \wedge
\langle\bar 0,U\rangle \in c \wedge \langle\bar 1,H\rangle \in c \wedge
\langle\bar 2,S\rangle \in c \wedge \langle\bar 3,D\rangle \in c.
$$

**Closure under pairs and unions**:

$$
\mathrm{PUCl}(U)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \forall x \in U\ \forall y \in U\
\bigl(\{x,y\} \in U \wedge \textstyle\bigcup x \in U\bigr).
$$

**The concatenated assignment** (the $a_x$ of (9.1)):

$$
\mathrm{Cons}(x,a,b)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\forall p\ \bigl(p \in b \iff p = \langle \bar 0, x\rangle \vee
\exists j\,\exists y\ (\langle j,y\rangle \in a \wedge p = \langle j^+, y\rangle)\bigr).
$$

**Definition inputs**:

$$
\mathrm{DefInp}(X,e,a)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Form}(e) \wedge \mathrm{Seq}_X(a) \wedge
\forall j \in w\ \forall k \in w\ \bigl(k = j^+ \to \neg\,\mathrm{NotFree}(k,e) \to j \in \mathrm{dom}(a)\bigr).
$$

**Enumeration of the definable subsets** (condition 4):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{DefEnum}(U,H_\xi,S_\xi,D_\xi)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
&\mathrm{Fun}(D_\xi)\ \wedge \\
&\forall k\ \Bigl(\exists b\ \langle k,b\rangle \in D_\xi \iff
 \exists e \in h\ \exists a \in U\ \bigl(k = \langle e,a\rangle \wedge \mathrm{DefInp}(H_\xi,e,a)\bigr)\Bigr) \wedge \\
&\forall e\,\forall a\,\forall b\ \Bigl(\langle\langle e,a\rangle,b\rangle \in D_\xi \to
 b \subseteq H_\xi\ \wedge \\
&\qquad \forall x \in H_\xi\ \bigl(x \in b \iff
 \exists a_x \in U\ (\mathrm{Cons}(x,a,a_x) \wedge \langle e,a_x\rangle \in S_\xi)\bigr)\Bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

**The clauses for each stage.** Function values cannot be written as terms, so the value is
universally quantified.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{SatCl}(U,H,S,\xi) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\forall H_\xi\ \bigl(\langle\xi,H_\xi\rangle \in H \to \forall S_\xi\ (\langle\xi,S_\xi\rangle \in S \to \mathrm{SatCode}(H_\xi,U,S_\xi))\bigr), \\
\mathrm{DefCl}(U,H,S,D,\xi) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\forall H_\xi\,\forall S_\xi\,\forall D_\xi\ \bigl(\cdots \to \mathrm{DefEnum}(U,H_\xi,S_\xi,D_\xi)\bigr), \\
\mathrm{SuccCl}(H,D,\xi) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\forall D_\xi\ \bigl(\langle\xi,D_\xi\rangle \in D \to \forall H' (\langle\xi^+,H'\rangle \in H \to \mathrm{Ran}(D_\xi) = H')\bigr), \\
\mathrm{LimCl}(H,\lambda) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\lambda \ne \emptyset \to (\forall \zeta \in \lambda\ \zeta^+ \in \lambda) \to
\forall H_\lambda\ \bigl(\langle\lambda,H_\lambda\rangle \in H \to \\
&\qquad (\forall x \in H_\lambda\ \exists \zeta \in \lambda\ \exists H_\zeta\ (\langle\zeta,H_\zeta\rangle \in H \wedge x \in H_\zeta)) \\
&\qquad \wedge\ (\forall \zeta \in \lambda\ \forall H_\zeta\ (\langle\zeta,H_\zeta\rangle \in H \to H_\zeta \subseteq H_\lambda))\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The two conjuncts of $\mathrm{LimCl}$ are the $\subseteq$ and $\supseteq$ of (9.3).

**The $L$-hierarchy code**:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ \exists U\,H\,S\,D\ \Bigl(
&\mathrm{FourCode}(c,U,H,S,D) \\
\wedge\ &\bigl(\mathrm{Ord}(\eta) \wedge \mathrm{Trans}(U) \wedge \eta,M,H,S,D,w \in U \wedge \mathrm{PUCl}(U)\bigr) \\
\wedge\ &\bigl(\mathrm{Fun}(H) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(H)=\eta^+ \wedge \mathrm{Fun}(S) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(S)=\eta \\
&\quad \wedge\ \mathrm{Fun}(D) \wedge \mathrm{Dom}(D)=\eta \wedge \langle\bar 0,\bar 0\rangle \in H\bigr) \\
\wedge\ &\forall \xi \in \eta\ \mathrm{SatCl}(U,H,S,\xi)
\ \wedge\ \forall \xi \in \eta\ \mathrm{DefCl}(U,H,S,D,\xi) \\
\wedge\ &\forall \xi \in \eta\ \mathrm{SuccCl}(H,D,\xi)
\ \wedge\ (\forall \lambda \in \eta\ \mathrm{LimCl}(H,\lambda)) \wedge \mathrm{LimCl}(H,\eta) \\
\wedge\ &\langle\eta,M\rangle \in H \Bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

There are two claims to prove.

1. $\Delta_0$-ness. $\mathrm{LCode}$ is a $\Delta_0$ predicate over the support
   $\{h,w,\eta,M,c\}$.
2. Soundness (Lemma 9.2). When $h = L_\omega$, $w = \omega$ and $\eta$ is an external ordinal,
   $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c) \Rightarrow M = L_\eta$.

### The proof in Lean

#### (a) $\Delta_0$-ness

Where the paper only says "the four leading existential quantifiers are bounded by
$\bigcup\bigcup c$", Lean writes the definition itself with **unbounded** $\exists U\,H\,S\,D$
and proves the equivalence with the bounded form as a lemma.

$$
\exists U\,H\,S\,D\ \bigl(\mathrm{FourCode}(c,U,H,S,D) \wedge P(U,H,S,D)\bigr)
\iff
\exists U \in \textstyle\bigcup\bigcup c\ \cdots \exists D \in \bigcup\bigcup c\
\bigl(\mathrm{FourCode} \wedge P\bigr)
$$

The rightward direction descends two levels from $\langle\bar i,X\rangle \in c$ via
$\{\bar i,X\} \in \bigcup c$ to $X \in \bigcup\bigcup c$. The leftward direction just drops the
bounds.

The $\Delta_0$-ness of each clause is assembled from the following pieces.

- $\mathrm{Ord}(\eta)$, $\mathrm{Trans}(U)$, $x \in U$, $\mathrm{PUCl}(U)$
- $\mathrm{Fun}(X)$, $\mathrm{Dom}(X)=d$, $\mathrm{Ran}(X)=r$, $\langle x,y\rangle \in X$
- $\mathrm{SatCode}$ ($\Delta_0$ by §8), $\mathrm{Form}$ ([P-01](P-01.md)),
  $\mathrm{NotFree}$ ([P-02](P-02.md))
- $\mathrm{Cons}(x,a,b)$: the $\forall p$ is bounded by splitting it into the side inside $b$
  and the side outside $b$

The second conjunct of $\mathrm{DefEnum}$,
$\forall k(\exists b\,\langle k,b\rangle \in D_\xi \iff \cdots)$, contains an unbounded
$\forall k$ and $\exists b$, so it is bounded by separating the $\Rightarrow$ and $\Leftarrow$
directions.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Rightarrow:\ &\forall p \in D_\xi\ \forall q \in p\ \forall k \in q\ \forall q' \in p\ \forall b \in q'\
\bigl(p = \langle k,b\rangle \to \exists r \in k\ \exists e \in r\ \exists r' \in k\ \exists a \in r'\ (\cdots)\bigr) \\
\Leftarrow:\ &\forall e \in h\ \forall a \in U\ \bigl(\mathrm{DefInp}(H_\xi,e,a) \to
\exists p \in D_\xi\ \exists q \in p\ \exists b \in q\ (p = \langle\langle e,a\rangle,b\rangle)\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

Extracting $e$ and $a$ from $k = \langle e,a\rangle$ is again a two-level $\in$ descent. The
$\forall H_\xi$ in the stage clauses such as $\mathrm{SatCl}$ has
$\langle\xi,H_\xi\rangle \in H$ as its antecedent, so it is bounded by $H$ using the rewriting
lemma of [P-01](P-01.md).

Finally put $\forall \xi \in \eta$ on top. The support becomes $\{h,w,\eta,M,c\}$.

#### (b) Soundness

Let $\eta$ be an external ordinal and assume $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$. First, since the
domain of $H$ is $\eta^+$, for every ordinal $\alpha \le \eta$ there is $H_\alpha$ with
$\langle\alpha,H_\alpha\rangle \in H$.

The claim is

$$
\forall \xi \le \eta\ \ \langle\xi, L_\xi\rangle \in H,
$$

shown by transfinite induction on $\xi$ (three cases: zero, successor, limit).

**$\xi = 0$ case:** the conjunct $\langle\bar 0,\bar 0\rangle \in H$ of $\mathrm{LCode}$ is the
conclusion as it stands. Since $\bar 0 = \emptyset$ and $L_0 = \emptyset$, we get
$\langle 0, L_0\rangle \in H$.

**$\xi = \zeta+1$ case:** $\zeta < \eta$, so $\zeta \in \eta$. From the domain conditions take
$\langle\zeta,S_\zeta\rangle \in S$ and $\langle\zeta,D_\zeta\rangle \in D$. Also, from
$\zeta+1 \le \eta$ take $H'$ with $\langle\zeta^+,H'\rangle \in H$. The induction hypothesis is
$\langle\zeta,L_\zeta\rangle \in H$. Now

- applying $\mathrm{SatCl}$ with $\xi=\zeta$ and $H_\zeta = L_\zeta$ gives
  $\mathrm{SatCode}(L_\zeta,U,S_\zeta)$,
- applying $\mathrm{DefCl}$ likewise gives $\mathrm{DefEnum}(U,L_\zeta,S_\zeta,D_\zeta)$,
- applying $\mathrm{SuccCl}$ gives $\mathrm{Ran}(D_\zeta) = H'$.

Next show

$$
y \in \mathrm{Ran}(D_\zeta) \iff y \in \mathrm{Def}(L_\zeta).
$$

$\Rightarrow$: if $y \in \mathrm{Ran}(D_\zeta)$ then
$\langle\langle e,a\rangle,y\rangle \in D_\zeta$, and the third conjunct of $\mathrm{DefEnum}$
gives $y \subseteq L_\zeta$ and

$$
\forall x \in L_\zeta\ \bigl(x \in y \iff \exists a_x \in U\ (\mathrm{Cons}(x,a,a_x) \wedge \langle e,a_x\rangle \in S_\zeta)\bigr).
$$

By correctness of $\mathrm{SatCode}$ (the theorem corresponding to Lemma 8.4),
$\langle e,a_x\rangle \in S_\zeta$ is equivalent to $(L_\zeta,\in) \models \varphi[a_x]$, and
since $a_x$ is the assignment putting $x$ at the front and shifting $a$ back, this means
$y = \{x \in L_\zeta : (L_\zeta,\in) \models \varphi[x,a]\}$. $\Leftarrow$ retraces the same
equivalence in reverse. Since the second conjunct of $\mathrm{DefEnum}$ says the domain is
**exactly** $\{\langle e,a\rangle : \mathrm{DefInp}\}$, it also follows that the input
corresponding to each element of $\mathrm{Def}(L_\zeta)$ is indeed in the domain.

Hence $H' = \mathrm{Ran}(D_\zeta) = \mathrm{Def}(L_\zeta) = L_{\zeta+1}$, giving
$\langle\zeta^+, L_{\zeta+1}\rangle \in H$. Since $\zeta^+$ is the von Neumann form of
$\zeta+1$, this is the conclusion.

**$\xi$ a limit:** use $\mathrm{LimCl}(H,\xi)$. Its two hypotheses, $\xi \ne \emptyset$ and
$\forall\zeta \in \xi\ \zeta^+ \in \xi$, follow from $\xi$ being a limit. Taking
$\langle\xi,H_\xi\rangle \in H$, the two conjuncts of $\mathrm{LimCl}$ give

$$
H_\xi \subseteq \bigcup_{\zeta<\xi} L_\zeta \quad\text{and}\quad
\forall \zeta<\xi\ L_\zeta \subseteq H_\xi
$$

(substituting the induction hypothesis $\langle\zeta,L_\zeta\rangle \in H$). Hence
$H_\xi = \bigcup_{\zeta<\xi} L_\zeta = L_\xi$.

Taking $\xi = \eta$, from $\langle\eta,M\rangle \in H$ and $H$ being a function, $M = L_\eta$.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There are three differences.

### (i) Formula codes are hereditarily finite sets rather than natural numbers

The paper's condition 4 writes $\{\langle e,a\rangle : e \in \omega,\ a \in U,\ \dots\}$,
whereas Lean has $\exists e \in h$ ($h = L_\omega$). Since codes are not natural numbers, they
cannot be bounded by $\omega$. The reason is the same as in [P-01](P-01.md).

The range of quantification itself cannot be narrowed. The first conjunct of $\mathrm{DefInp}$
is $\mathrm{Form}(e)$, so the actual extension of $\exists e \in h$ is
$\{\langle e,a\rangle : e \text{ a formula code},\ a \in U,\ \mathrm{DefInp}\}$, which agrees
with the set in the paper's condition 4. Here $h$ is the same "place where codes live" as the
paper's $\omega$.

### (ii) There are two fixed parameters

The paper's $\mathrm{LCode}$ has $\omega$ as its only parameter ($w$ is suppressed by the
declaration in §8). Lean has the two $(h,w) = (L_\omega,\omega)$, and the support of the
$\Delta_0$-ness becomes $\{h,w,\eta,M,c\}$. This is because the place where variable indices
live, $w$, and the place where codes live, $h$, are the same $\omega$ in the paper but
different in Lean. The reason $h$ cannot be removed is the same as in [P-01](P-01.md).

### (iii) Function values are not written as terms

The paper writes function applications $H(\xi), S(\xi), D(\xi)$ in term position. Lean cannot
use function symbols as terms, so it universally quantifies the values as
$\forall H_\xi\ (\langle\xi,H_\xi\rangle \in H \to \cdots)$. That $H$ is a function is
guaranteed by a separate conjunct, so the extensions agree. The $a_x$ of (9.1) is the same: a
term in the paper, $\exists a_x \in U$ in Lean. For that one, unique existence is proved
separately.

This is due to the absence of a framework for writing $\Delta_0$-definable functions in term
position. The same difference appears for $\mathrm{Vars}/\mathrm{Body}$ in
[P-12](P-12.md).
