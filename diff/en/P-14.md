# P-14: Lemma 15.5, correctness of $\mathrm{Rel}_k$

## The paper

### Statement

We first quote Definitions 15.2 and 15.4 and Lemma 15.3, on which it rests.

Definition 15.2.

$$
\mathrm{St}_k(\xi) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\forall M\,\forall c\ \bigl(\mathrm{LCode}(\xi,M,c) \to \mathrm{TV}_{k+2}(M)\bigr). \tag{15.2}
$$

By Lemma 14.3, $\mathrm{St}_k \in \hat\Pi_{k+2}$.

Lemma 15.3 (correctness of $\mathrm{St}_k$). If $\xi < \theta$ are both admissible ordinals,
then

$$
L_\theta \models \mathrm{St}_k(\xi) \iff \xi \lhd_k \theta. \tag{15.3}
$$

Let $\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$ be the relativization restricting all unbounded quantifiers to $M$.
This is $\Delta_0$.

Definition 15.4.

$$
\mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta) \ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Ord}(\xi) \wedge \mathrm{Ord}(\eta) \wedge \xi < \eta \wedge
\exists M\,\exists c\ \bigl(\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c) \wedge \mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M\bigr). \tag{15.4}
$$

Hence $\mathrm{Rel}_k \in \hat\Sigma_1$.

Lemma 15.5 (correctness of $\mathrm{Rel}_k$).

1. If $\xi < \eta$ are both admissible ordinals then
   $\mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta) \iff \xi \lhd_k \eta$.
2. If $\xi < \eta < \theta$ are all admissible ordinals then
   $L_\theta \models \mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta) \iff \xi \lhd_k \eta$.

### Proof

(1) Since $\mathrm{ZFC}$ contains $\mathrm{KP}$, Lemma 10.5(2) can be applied in the external
universe to obtain a code satisfying $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$. By Lemma 9.2, necessarily
$M = L_\eta$. Moreover $\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$ is the fixed formula $\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)$ with all
its unbounded quantifiers restricted to $M$, so

$$\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^{L_\eta} \iff L_\eta \models \mathrm{St}_k(\xi).$$

Applying Lemma 15.3 with $\theta = \eta$, this is equivalent to $\xi \lhd_k \eta$. Conversely,
every witness $M,c$ of $\mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta)$ also denotes $L_\eta$ by Lemma 9.2, so the
same equivalence holds in the other direction.

(2) Inside $L_\theta$, a code for $L_\eta$ exists by Corollary 10.6. Since $\mathrm{LCode}$ and
$\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$ are $\Delta_0$, every internal witness code is correct externally as
well, and by Lemma 9.2 its $M$ is the external $L_\eta$. Hence

$$L_\theta \models \mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta) \iff L_\eta \models \mathrm{St}_k(\xi) \iff \xi \lhd_k \eta,$$

the last equivalence by Lemma 15.3.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

**Definition 15.2**:

$$
\mathrm{St}_k^{D}(\xi)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\forall M \in D\ \forall c \in D\ \bigl(\mathrm{LCode}(\xi,M,c) \to \mathrm{TV}_{k+2}^{D}(M)\bigr).
$$

**The fixed formula.** $\mathrm{St}_k$ is a $\hat\Pi_{k+2}$ predicate, so a formula
representing it exists. One such is chosen and written $\sigma_k$. The variable assignment is
$0 \mapsto h$, $1 \mapsto w$, $2 \mapsto \xi$, and the variable $3$ is arranged not to occur
among the bound variables of $\sigma_k$.

$$
\mathrm{fv}(\sigma_k) \subseteq \{0,1,2\}, \qquad 3 \notin \mathrm{vars}(\sigma_k),
$$

$$
D \text{ a good domain}, \ v \text{ taking values in } D \text{ on } \{0,1,2\}\ \Longrightarrow\
\bigl(D \models \sigma_k[v] \iff \mathrm{St}_k^{D}(v(2))\bigr).
$$

**The relativized formula itself** (the paper's $\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$):

$$
\sigma_k^{M} := \mathrm{relTo}(3,\ \sigma_k)
$$

that is, $\sigma_k$ with its unbounded quantifiers restricted to the variable $3$ (12.2(1)-d of
[P-10](P-10.md)). Put

$$
\mathrm{stVal}(h,w,\xi,M) := (0 \mapsto h,\ 1 \mapsto w,\ 2 \mapsto \xi,\ \text{otherwise} \mapsto M).
$$

**Definition 15.4**:

$$
\mathrm{Rel}_k^{D}(\xi,\eta)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Ord}(\xi) \wedge \mathrm{Ord}(\eta) \wedge \xi \in \eta \wedge
\exists M \in D\ \exists c \in D\
\bigl(\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c) \wedge D \models \sigma_k^{M}[\mathrm{stVal}(h,w,\xi,M)]\bigr).
$$

The fourth conjunct is **unconditional**. It agrees with (15.4) as printed.

There are three claims to prove.

**(0) The bridge.** If $M$ is contained in $D$, transitive, nonempty, and $h,w,\xi \in M$, then

$$
D \models \sigma_k^{M}[\mathrm{stVal}(h,w,\xi,M)] \iff \mathrm{St}_k^{(\cdot\in M)}(\xi).
$$

**(1) The external version** (Lemma 15.5(1)). If $\xi < \eta$ are both good ordinals then

$$
\mathrm{Rel}_k^{V}(\xi,\eta) \iff L_\xi \prec^*_{k+2} L_\eta.
$$

**(2) The internal version** (Lemma 15.5(2)). If $\xi < \eta < \theta$ are all good ordinals
then

$$
\mathrm{Rel}_k^{L_\theta}(\xi,\eta) \iff L_\xi \prec^*_{k+2} L_\eta.
$$

A "good ordinal" bundles admissibility together with the properties used in this section, such
as the existence of an $L$-code.

**Being $\Delta_0$.** $D \models \sigma_k^{M}[\cdots]$ is a $\Delta_0$ predicate over the
support $\{h,w,x,m\}$. **There are no side conditions.**

### The proof in Lean

#### (0) The bridging lemma

Use the semantic lemma for relativization (12.2(1)-d of [P-10](P-10.md)). Under the five
conditions that $3 \notin \mathrm{vars}(\sigma_k)$, that the value of variable $3$ in
$\mathrm{stVal}$ is $M$, that $M \subseteq D$, that $M$ is transitive, and that the values on
$\mathrm{fv}(\sigma_k)$ lie in $M$,

$$
D \models \mathrm{relTo}(3,\sigma_k)[v] \iff (\cdot \in M) \models \sigma_k[v].
$$

Applying the specification of $\sigma_k$ ($M$ a good domain, $v$ taking values in $M$ on
$\{0,1,2\}$) to the right-hand side gives $\mathrm{St}_k^{(\cdot\in M)}(\xi)$. That $M$ is a
good domain follows from transitivity and nonemptiness.

#### Being $\Delta_0$

$\sigma_k^{M}$ is a single fixed formula, so its satisfaction is a $\Delta_0$ predicate as it
stands. To move the support from $\{0,1,2,3\}$ to $\{h,w,x,m\}$, use the renaming

$$
\rho: 0 \mapsto h,\ 1 \mapsto w,\ 2 \mapsto x,\ 3 \mapsto m,\ i \mapsto i + K\ (i \ge 4)
$$

($K$ large enough, $\rho$ injective). Combine the facts that being $\Delta_0$ is preserved by
renaming, that the set of free variables goes to its image, and that satisfaction corresponds
to composition of the valuation.

**No side condition is needed here.** This agrees with the paper's Lemma 15.5(2), which says
unconditionally "since $\mathrm{LCode}$ and $\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$ are $\Delta_0$".

#### (1) The external version

**$\Rightarrow$.** Take witnesses $M,c$ from $\mathrm{Rel}_k^{V}(\xi,\eta)$. From
$\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$ and Lemma 9.2 ([P-03](P-03.md)), $M = L_\eta$. Apply the bridging
lemma to get $\mathrm{St}_k^{(\cdot\in L_\eta)}(\xi)$, that is,
$L_\eta \models \mathrm{St}_k(\xi)$. Apply Lemma 15.3 with $\theta := \eta$ to get
$L_\xi \prec^*_{k+2} L_\eta$.

This is the part corresponding to the paper's "conversely, every witness $M,c$ of
$\mathrm{Rel}_k(\xi,\eta)$ also denotes $L_\eta$ by Lemma 9.2".

**$\Leftarrow$.** Build an $L$-code for $L_\eta$ in the external universe. Here the paper writes
"since $\mathrm{ZFC}$ contains $\mathrm{KP}$, apply Lemma 10.5(2) in the external universe",
but Lean's Lemma 10.5(2) ((2) of [P-08](P-08.md)) is a claim inside $L_\theta$, so it
**cannot be applied as it stands**. A separate existence theorem for $V$ is provided. With it,
take $M,c$, check $M = L_\eta$, and use the bridging lemma and Lemma 15.3 in the other
direction.

#### (2) The internal version

The structure is the same as (1), with the domain $D$ changed from $V$ to $L_\theta$.

**$\Rightarrow$.** From witnesses $M,c \in L_\theta$, get $M = L_\eta$, then the bridge, then
Lemma 15.3. The hypothesis "$M \subseteq D$" of the bridging lemma follows from transitivity of
$L_\theta$ and $L_\eta \in L_\theta$.

**$\Leftarrow$.** Take $c \in L_\theta$ from the existence of an $L$-code inside $L_\theta$
((2) of [P-08](P-08.md)).

Where the paper goes through $\Delta_0$ absoluteness ("since $\mathrm{LCode}$ and
$\mathrm{St}_k(\xi)^M$ are $\Delta_0$, every internal witness code is correct externally as
well"), Lean does not. Both $\mathrm{LCode}$ and the satisfaction of $\sigma_k^{M}$ are written
as external predicates from the start, and $D$ is used only to narrow the range of
quantification. $M = L_\eta$ comes from applying Lemma 9.2 externally as it stands.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

There is one difference: in the proof of Lemma 15.5(1), the $L$-code in the external universe
is built by a separate construction.

The paper writes

since $\mathrm{ZFC}$ contains $\mathrm{KP}$, Lemma 10.5(2) can be applied in the external
universe to obtain a code satisfying $\mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c)$

and so reuses a single lemma in two universes. Lean's Lemma 10.5(2) is the semantic claim "if
$\theta$ is admissible and $\eta < \theta$ then $M,c$ exist inside $L_\theta$", which cannot be
applied to $V$ as it stands. A separate existence theorem for $V$ is written, so the same
content is proved twice.

This is a direct consequence of (i) of [P-04](P-04.md). Had it been stated in the form
$\mathrm{KP} \vdash \forall\eta(\mathrm{Ord}(\eta) \to \exists M\exists c\ \mathrm{LCode}(\eta,M,c))$,
it would be enough to apply soundness to $V$. That would require a proof system for
$\mathrm{KP}$ (an inductive type of derivations, structural induction over derivations,
soundness); by the estimate in [P-04](P-04.md), (a)+(b) would be 1,500 to 2,500 lines and
turning §10 into internal derivations more than 3,000.

The line of the proof also differs in one place. The paper's (2) uses $\Delta_0$ absoluteness
("every internal witness code is correct externally as well"), whereas Lean does not. Both
$\mathrm{LCode}$ and the satisfaction of $\sigma_k^{M}$ are external predicates from the start,
and $D$ is used only to narrow the range of quantification. This too is the flip side of the
absence of $\mathrm{KP} \vdash$: there is no inside/outside distinction to begin with.
