# P-11: Lemma 13.3, correctness of $\Delta_0$ truth

## The paper

### Statement

Lemma 13.3 (correctness of $\Delta_0$ truth). Let $\theta$ be an admissible ordinal and
$W = L_\theta$. If $e,a \in W$, $e$ codes a $\Delta_0$ formula $\delta$, and $a$ is an
appropriate assignment, then

$$
W \models \mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(e,a) \iff
W \models \mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(e,a) \iff
W \models \delta[a]. \tag{13.2}
$$

The same equivalence holds in the external universe $V$.

We also quote Definition 13.2, on which it rests.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(e,a) &:\Longleftrightarrow
e \text{ is a } \Delta_0 \text{ code} \wedge \exists A \text{ transitive}\ \exists U,T\
\bigl(\mathrm{Asn}_A(e,a) \wedge a \in U \wedge \mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T) \wedge \langle e,a\rangle \in T\bigr), \\
\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(e,a) &:\Longleftrightarrow \text{its dual}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Proof

First work inside $W = L_\theta$. By Lemma 13.1, $\theta$ is a limit greater than $\omega$.
Take $\rho$ with $\omega+1 < \rho < \theta$ and $\rho+1 < \theta$ that contains $a$ and its
range. Putting $A = L_\rho$, the set $A$ is transitive, contains all the values of the
assignment $a$, and $A \in L_{\rho+1} \subseteq W$.

Since $W \models \mathrm{KP}$, apply Lemma 10.5(1) inside $W$ to $A$ and obtain $U,T \in W$
with $W \models \mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$. As $\mathrm{SatCode}$ is $\Delta_0$ and $W$ is
transitive, $\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$ holds externally as well. By Lemma 8.4,

$$\langle e,a\rangle \in T \iff (A,\in) \models \delta[a]. \tag{13.3}$$

Since $A \subseteq W$ are both transitive and $\delta$ is $\Delta_0$,

$$(A,\in) \models \delta[a] \iff W \models \delta[a]. \tag{13.4}$$

(13.3) and (13.4) give the equivalence for the positive form.

Now the negative form. Suppose $A',U',T' \in W$ satisfy the antecedent of the negative form.
By transitivity of $W$ and $\Delta_0$ absoluteness, $A'$ is transitive and
$\mathrm{SatCode}(A',U',T')$ holds externally as well. By Lemma 8.4 and $\Delta_0$
absoluteness,

$$\langle e,a\rangle \in T' \iff A' \models \delta[a] \iff W \models \delta[a].$$

Hence if $W \models \delta[a]$ then every candidate code judges it true. Conversely, if
$W \not\models \delta[a]$ then the $A,U,T \in W$ constructed at the start are a counterexample
to the universal condition of the negative form. This gives (13.2).

In the external universe, take as a transitive set containing the assigned values

$$
A = \mathrm{TC}\bigl(\mathrm{ran}(a) \cup \{\mathrm{ran}(a)\}\bigr)
$$

and construct the satisfaction code of Lemma 8.4 inside $\mathrm{ZFC}$. The same absoluteness
argument applies verbatim.

## Lean

### The statement in Lean

Definition 13.2 reads as follows. Let $D$ be the domain, $h = L_\omega$ and $w = \omega$.

$$
\mathrm{Mat}(e,a,A,U,T)\ :\Longleftrightarrow\
\mathrm{Trans}(A) \wedge \mathrm{Asn}_A(e,a) \wedge a \in U \wedge \mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T) \wedge \langle e,a\rangle \in T,
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(e,a) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\Delta_0\mathrm{Code}(e) \wedge \exists A \in D\ \exists U \in D\ \exists T \in D\ \mathrm{Mat}(e,a,A,U,T), \\
\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(e,a) &:\Longleftrightarrow
\Delta_0\mathrm{Code}(e) \wedge \forall A \in D\ \forall U \in D\ \forall T \in D\
\bigl(\mathrm{Mat}'(e,a,A,U,T) \to \langle e,a\rangle \in T\bigr)
\end{aligned}
$$

(where $\mathrm{Mat}'$ is $\mathrm{Mat}$ with the last conjunct $\langle e,a\rangle \in T$
removed). $\Delta_0\mathrm{Code}(e)$ is the paper's "$e$ codes a $\Delta_0$ formula"; without
it, $\mathrm{Tr}^-$ would be vacuously true for an $e$ that is not a code.

Lemma 13.3 is written as two claims.

**(1) Inside $W$**: let $\theta$ be admissible and $W = L_\theta$. For a $\Delta_0$ formula
$\delta$ and $a \in W$ such that $a$ is a standard finite sequence with values in $W$ covering
the free variables of $\delta$,

$$
\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}^+_{\Delta_0}(\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a) \iff W \models \delta[a]\bigr)
\ \wedge\
\bigl(\mathrm{Tr}^-_{\Delta_0}(\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a) \iff W \models \delta[a]\bigr).
$$

**(2) Inside $V$**: for a $\Delta_0$ formula $\delta$ and a standard finite sequence $a$
covering the free variables of $\delta$, the same equivalence holds with the domain taken to be
$V$ (everything).

Since $e$ is the code of an external standard formula $\delta$, the paper's hypothesis
"$e \in W$" is automatic (a code is an element of $L_\omega \subseteq W$). The hypothesis "$a$
is an appropriate assignment" is spelled out as "$a$ covers the free variables of $\delta$".

### The proof in Lean

#### (1) Inside $W = L_\theta$

**The choice of $A$ differs.** The paper takes $\rho$ with $\omega+1 < \rho < \theta$ and
$\rho+1 < \theta$ and puts $A = L_\rho$. Lean directly takes "a stage of the hierarchy
containing $a$". Since $a \in L_\theta$, there is $\xi < \theta$ with $a \in L_\xi$, and by
transitivity of $L_\theta$ the $\xi$ can be chosen so that the values of $a$ also lie in
$L_\xi$. Put $A := L_\xi$. The two conditions on $\rho$ ($\omega+1 < \rho$ and
$\rho+1 < \theta$) are not used. That $A$ is transitive and $A \in L_\theta$ follows from the
properties of the $L$ hierarchy.

**Obtaining the satisfaction code.** The paper takes two steps, "since
$W \models \mathrm{KP}$, apply Lemma 10.5(1) inside $W$ and move it outside by $\Delta_0$
absoluteness". Lean uses (1) of [P-08](P-08.md) directly (if $\theta$ is admissible and
$A \in L_\theta$ then there are $U,T \in L_\theta$ with $\mathrm{SatCode}(A,U,T)$), and so
**never moves between the inside and the outside**, because $\mathrm{SatCode}$ is given as an
external predicate from the start.

$a \in U$ follows from Lemma 8.2 (a finite sequence inside a $\mathrm{PUCl}$ set), since
$\mathrm{SatCode}$ includes transitivity of $U$, $\mathrm{PUCl}$-ness of $U$, and
$A,\omega \in U$.

**The key lemma (combining (13.3) and (13.4)).** The following is shown first.

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\forall A',U',T'\ \bigl(\mathrm{Trans}(A') \wedge A' \in L_\theta \wedge
\mathrm{Asn}_{A'}(\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a) \wedge \mathrm{SatCode}(A',U',T')\bigr) \\
&\qquad \to \bigl(\langle\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a\rangle \in T' \iff W \models \delta[a]\bigr).
\end{aligned}
$$

The proof has two steps. First, correctness of the satisfaction code (Lemma 8.4) gives

$$
\langle\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a\rangle \in T' \iff (A',\in) \models \delta[a]
$$

(the paper's (13.3)). Then $\Delta_0$ absoluteness is used **twice**. Since $\delta$ is
$\Delta_0$, $A'$ is transitive and the values of $a$ lie in $A'$,

$$
(A',\in) \models \delta[a] \iff V \models \delta[a],
$$

and likewise, since $W$ is transitive and the values of $a$ lie in $W$,

$$
(W,\in) \models \delta[a] \iff V \models \delta[a].
$$

Joining the two gives the paper's (13.4). The paper uses the inclusion $A \subseteq W$ to get
$(A,\in)\models\delta[a] \iff W \models\delta[a]$ in one step, whereas Lean compares each of
them with $V$. Since it does not assume $A' \subseteq W$, the key lemma also applies to an
arbitrary candidate $A'$ of $\mathrm{Tr}^-$.

**The positive form.** $\Rightarrow$: extract candidates $A',U',T' \in L_\theta$ from
$\mathrm{Tr}^+$ and apply the key lemma. $\Leftarrow$: use the $A = L_\xi$, $U$, $T$
constructed above as witnesses.

**The negative form.** $\Rightarrow$: instantiate the universal quantifier of $\mathrm{Tr}^-$
at the $(L_\xi,U,T)$ constructed above to get
$\langle\ulcorner\delta\urcorner,a\rangle \in T$, then apply the key lemma to get
$W \models \delta[a]$. $\Leftarrow$: use the key lemma in the other direction for an arbitrary
candidate $A',U',T'$.

Where the paper argues by contraposition ("if $W \not\models \delta[a]$ then the $A,U,T$
constructed at the start are a counterexample"), Lean writes the $\Rightarrow$ direction
directly. The content is the same.

#### (2) The external universe $V$

The paper takes $A = \mathrm{TC}(\mathrm{ran}(a) \cup \{\mathrm{ran}(a)\})$. Lean obtains "a
transitive set $A$ containing $a$" from a separate lemma (taken from the rank of the range).
All that is needed is that $A$ is transitive and contains every value of $a$; minimality is not
used.

Next, a satisfaction code $(U,T)$ for $A$ is constructed inside $\mathrm{ZFC}$. This is the $V$
version of (1) of [P-08](P-08.md), built with the power set and Separation rather than with
$L_\theta$.

From there on the same key lemma as in (1) is used, with domain $D := V$ and $W := V$. Since
$V$ is transitive, $\Delta_0$ absoluteness is available in the same form.

## Why it could not be written as in the paper

The statement itself is written as in the paper. Both of the paper's hypotheses, that $\delta$
is a $\Delta_0$ formula and that $a$ is an appropriate assignment, are present, and the two
equivalences in the conclusion agree with (13.2). The paper's hypothesis $e \in W$ is automatic
because $e$ is the code of an external standard formula $\delta$ and codes are elements of
$L_\omega \subseteq W$.

The line of the proof differs in three places. None of them changes the conclusion.

### (i) The choice of $A$

The paper takes $\rho$ with $\omega+1 < \rho < \theta$ and $\rho+1 < \theta$ and puts
$A = L_\rho$, whereas Lean directly takes the stage $L_\xi$ containing $a$. The two conditions
on $\rho$ are not used.

### (ii) No round trip between the inside and the outside

The paper applies Lemma 10.5(1) inside $W$ and then moves outside by $\Delta_0$ absoluteness,
whereas (1) of [P-08](P-08.md) in Lean is an external claim from the start, so there is no
round trip. Since there is no claim inside $\mathrm{KP}$, there is no inside/outside
distinction either. This is the flip side of (i) of [P-04](P-04.md).

### (iii) How $\Delta_0$ absoluteness is used

The paper goes from the inclusion $A \subseteq W$ to
$(A,\in)\models\delta[a] \iff W\models\delta[a]$ in one step, whereas Lean compares $A$ and
$W$ each with $V$ and joins the two. Since the form does not assume $A' \subseteq W$, the same
lemma also applies to an arbitrary candidate of $\mathrm{Tr}^-$.
